Collecting duty-free orders at Incheon Airport's Terminal 2 is where a lot of first-time departures from Korea lose twenty minutes. The app says "towards gate 225", so you walk to gate 225 and find a gate. The collection hall is one floor down, and its entrance is a blue sign reading "Duty Free Pick Up".

What follows is the sequence in order: ordering, when to arrive, finding the pickup point, taking a number, collecting at the counter, and finally the rules on liquids and transfers. Queue times and counter staffing vary enormously between a weekday and a holiday.

## Before you fly: order on the app

Lotte, Shilla, Shinsegae and Hyundai each run their own duty-free app. Install and register before the trip, fill a cart, and at checkout enter the **departure flight number, date and terminal**. Pickup points are assigned by terminal, so an order for a T2 departure can only be collected at T2; get the terminal wrong and the order needs changing.

Most duty-free retailers require the order to be completed a set number of hours before departure, and limited sets sell out earlier than that. Ordering early is the safe move; the cut-off and purchase limits are on the order page.

The app then shows a collection point, usually phrased as "towards gate 225". **That is a direction, not an address** — the real location is on the airport's blue signs.

Two more things catch people out. Orders for departures from Gimpo, Busan or other airports are not collected at Incheon. And transit passengers who do not enter Korea normally book against their final departing flight, not the flight that brought them to Korea; with the wrong flight on the order, the counter cannot find it.

## How early: three hours, or one

Without a connection, arriving about three hours before departure is comfortable. That time covers check-in, bag drop, security, the walk to the pickup point and the queue, and the walk alone takes ten minutes or so from the T2 departure area. At holiday peaks the queue can run beyond 40 minutes.

Transit passengers do not clear immigration and walk to the pickup point inside the transit area, but should still allow **at least an hour before boarding**. On a tight connection, collect first and shop afterwards.

The pickup point is airside, past security; it cannot be reached from the public concourse and nobody can collect on your behalf. Arriving too early carries its own risk — counters open around their flight banks, so a very early arrival may find them shut. Opening hours follow the airport's notices.

## The pickup point is not gate 225

Look for the **Korean Traditional Culture Center**. It stands on level 3, faced in coloured timber slats, with the green Transfer Lounge sign beside it. The collection point is opposite that building — not at gate 225 itself.

![The blue "Duty Free Pick Up" sign on level 3, with an arrow towards the escalator](./images/1.webp)

Find the blue sign reading "면세품인도장 / DUTY FREE PICK UP / 免税品取货处" and follow the arrow to the escalator.

![A wall sign on level 3 pointing down the escalator to the collection hall](./images/2.webp)

Down one floor is the collection hall on 2F. T2's concourse is long: from security to here is usually a ten-minute walk, so do not estimate the timing from the gate number.

## Down on 2F: kiosks and numbers

The 2F hall is divided by duty-free company, and a row of self-service kiosks stands at the entrance — **one machine per retailer**, each carrying its company logo. Queue at the right one.

![A Lotte Duty Free kiosk with a Chinese-language screen; scan a passport to print a number](./images/3.webp)

The Lotte machine offers Korean, Chinese, English and Japanese. Follow the prompt, place the photo page of your passport on the scanner, and it prints a number ticket; if the scan fails, the passport number can be typed in. The ticket shows the retailer, the number and the time it was issued.

Check which zone the ticket belongs to, then wait in that zone. Screens above the hall scroll through the numbers being served; go up when yours appears, because a missed number usually means taking another.

## At the counter: passport, boarding pass, number

![The Lotte counters and number screens in the 2F collection hall](./images/4.webp)

Hand over **passport, boarding pass and number ticket** together. Staff check the order against the goods, scan each item, bag it, seal it, attach the paperwork and ask you to sign.

Check everything before stepping away: number of items, sizes, shades, gifts. Damage or a wrong item is dealt with at the counter on the spot, by exchange or refund; once you have left, it becomes a customer-service case.

Do not open the sealed bag early. Liquids go into a clear security bag with the receipt inside, and bag and receipt stay intact until boarding — there is another security check if you are transferring.

## Liquids, transfers and other traps

**Liquids and seals.** Duty-free liquids over 100ml must stay in the sealed security bag with the receipt. Security at a transfer will not accept a broken seal, and opened bottles may have to be surrendered.

**Check the transfer route first.** Some airports on a connecting itinerary do not accept duty-free security bags and may require the liquid to be checked in or abandoned. Before buying alcohol or cosmetics, confirm the rules at the airport where you change.

**The order is tied to the flight.** It is valid only on the departure day and at the stated terminal; there is no collection on the return leg. Uncollected orders follow each retailer's policy, usually refundable, as set out on the order page.

**Two useful extras.** The tax refund desk sits near gate 249; cash refunds come only in US dollars and yen, other currencies are handled by machine, and Alipay and WeChat Pay are accepted. Convenience stores, a fishcake shop, bubble tea and a bakery sit around gates 233 to 278, so stocking up after collection is cheaper than at the gate.
