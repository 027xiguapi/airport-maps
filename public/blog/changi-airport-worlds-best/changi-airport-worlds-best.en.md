The World Airport Awards were announced in London on 18 March, and Singapore Changi Airport (SIN) was named World's Best Airport for 2026 — the second year running and the 14th time it has taken the title.

For an airport that has already been ranked first 13 times, "again" is not the story. The breadth is. Alongside the top prize, Changi won World's Best Airport Dining, World's Best Airport Immigration Service, Best Airport in Asia and Best Airport 60–70 million passengers; Crowne Plaza Changi Airport, attached to Terminal 3, took both World's Best Airport Hotel and Best Airport Hotel in Asia. Five airport trophies and two hotel trophies from one awards programme is a rare sweep.

## 1. Five awards: what was actually won

Start with the list. The World Airport Awards are run by the UK consultancy Skytrax and based on passenger-satisfaction surveys covering 575 airports, which is why the programme has two kinds of prize: the overall ranking, and categories split by part of the journey or by airport size.

| Award | Winner |
| --- | --- |
| World's Best Airport | Changi Airport (14th time, second year running) |
| World's Best Airport Dining | Changi Airport |
| World's Best Airport Immigration Service | Changi Airport |
| Best Airport in Asia | Changi Airport |
| Best Airport 60–70 million passengers | Changi Airport |
| World's Best Airport Hotel | Crowne Plaza Changi Airport |
| Best Airport Hotel in Asia | Crowne Plaza Changi Airport |

Only one of the five airport awards is the overall title; the rest point at dining, immigration, region and size. Taking the top prize and four different category prizes in the same year suggests the strength is not one standout feature but a consistently high floor across the journey.

## 2. Why dining and immigration are the awards worth reading

In airport awards generally, "best airport" tends to be captured by architecture and retail space. Dining and immigration are harder operational measures.

Dining measures choice, price and consistency: whether a passenger with a two-hour connection can find a proper meal, whether the menus account for different diets, whether anything is still open around a late-night departure. That is not delivered by a single refurbishment; it takes years of tenant management and rent structures. Changi's reputation for food owes a lot to putting Singapore's hawker culture inside the terminals rather than offering only chain fast food.

Immigration is more direct still: it measures throughput at the desks, the clarity of signage and how staff handle people. For an airport built around transfers, this is the category transit passengers barely notice — but for anyone actually entering Singapore, it is the first gate after landing. Winning here suggests Changi puts as much effort into the human side of the process as into the hardware.

## 3. The transit model: turning a stopover into a stay

To see why these awards keep landing at Changi, look at its structure. The airport opened in 1981, has four terminals, is the home base of Singapore Airlines and is one of the most important interchange points on the Europe–Southeast Asia–Australia chain. A large share of its passengers never clear immigration; they simply walk from one gate to another.

That transit-heavy mix means the airport has to treat connection time as a product. The terminals are linked by an automated people mover and shuttle buses, and most connecting passengers do not need to enter Singapore, though a terminal change still deserves an hour of margin. Jewel Changi Airport, which opened in 2019, put the Rain Vortex, a forest landscape and retail between the terminals — an answer to the question of what a passenger with four hours to wait should do with them. The waterfall falling from the dome in the cover image is the clearest expression of that idea.

The tension in the model is that a destination airport has to serve two audiences at once. Transit passengers want a short, predictable walk; local visitors and passengers with time to kill want shops, restaurants and attractions. Changi keeps Jewel outside the transit area for most travellers, which protects the transfer flows but turns the waterfall into a detour rather than a shortcut. That is also why the immigration award matters commercially as much as operationally: the easier the airport is to enter and leave, the easier it is to sell a stopover rather than a connection.

Terminal 5, now under construction, is the next stage of the same model. In the short term passengers will notice the inter-terminal trains and signage; in the longer term it depends on whether the new building can consolidate transfer flows currently spread across four terminals.

## 4. The other list that week: Singapore Airlines third, Scoot fifth

Away from the airport, Singapore's airlines had a good week on a separate ranking. In AirlineRatings.com's World's Best Airline Awards 2026, Singapore Airlines placed third among full-service carriers, behind Qatar Airways and Cathay Pacific.

![The AirlineRatings.com World's Best Airline Awards 2026 banner, showing Qatar Airways, Lufthansa and HK Express aircraft](./images/1.webp)

The AirlineRatings judging panel singled out Singapore Airlines for balanced, strong performance across economy, premium economy, business and first class — the same logic as Changi's awards: not a single-cabin win, but consistency.

In the low-cost category, Scoot, part of the Singapore Airlines Group, placed fifth. Scoot has been expanding its Boeing 787 Dreamliner fleet and its network across Asia-Pacific and beyond, and the rating reflects a balance of price and service quality. For travellers, it means both a full-service and a low-cost option when starting from or connecting through Singapore.

## 5. What it means for travellers

Put the trophies back into an itinerary and each one points at a different part of the trip. If you are only connecting at Changi, the dining award and the overall title matter most: leave two to three hours and you can cross terminals for a proper local meal and still be back at the gate. Jewel is better suited to connections of four hours or more, but factor in immigration and re-screening if you plan to leave the transit area.

If you are entering Singapore, the immigration award is the relevant one. Clearance at Changi is usually faster than the regional average, but queues still build when holiday traffic and early-morning arrivals converge, and completing the electronic arrival card in advance saves time at the desk.

As for "world's best", treat it as an input rather than a guarantee. On the same list, Seoul Incheon was second, Tokyo Haneda third and Hong Kong International fourth, with London Heathrow 16th, Melbourne 21st and London Gatwick 30th. The gaps between those positions usually come down to a handful of specific processes. What decides whether your trip runs smoothly is still the schedule, the connection time and your documents.
