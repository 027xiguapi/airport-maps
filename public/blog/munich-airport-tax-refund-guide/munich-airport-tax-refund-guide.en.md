The counter-intuitive thing about a tax refund at Munich Airport is that the stamp is not available in one place only. One customs office sits before immigration, another after passport control, and picking the wrong side costs you a long walk back. First-time claimants worry about the language, but the signage and the counter notices are clear, and Global Blue prints its process as three steps and sticks it on the desk.

There is only one test for which route applies: are the refundable goods in your checked suitcase, or in your hand luggage? EU rules require customs to see the goods before they leave the EU. Once a suitcase is in the baggage system you cannot open it again, so the stamp has to happen before check-in; hand luggage travels with you, so its stamp comes after passport control.

What follows takes the two cases separately, then covers timing and paperwork. Positions below come from a 2026 claim; desks move, so check the airport website before you travel.

## 1. First, work out which of the two cases you are in

Answer this before anything else. Get it wrong and every later step is inverted: leave a checked-bag stamp until after passport control and customs cannot see the suitcase, so they must refuse; bring hand-luggage goods to the pre-immigration office and the goods are not with you. In practice the customs office, the Global Blue desk and the Reise Bank counter sit within a small area, and following the Zoll/Customs signs is enough.

| | Case A: goods in checked baggage | Case B: goods in hand luggage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When to get the stamp | After the boarding pass, before the bag is dropped | After both security and passport control |
| Where | The customs office (Zoll/Customs) beside the immigration queue | Near **gates H28 and H30**, after passport control |
| Where next | The Global Blue desk next to the customs office | The Global Blue counter near gate H30 |
| Main risk | The bag is already checked and cannot be inspected | Liquid and sharp-object limits apply first |

Both cases need the same things: passport, boarding pass, a completed form, the matching receipts, and goods that are **unused and still sealed**. German VAT on most goods is 19%, but you do not receive 19%: the refund company charges a fee, and the figure printed on the form is the figure you get.

One more rule catches people out: the refund belongs to the point where you last leave the EU. Flying home from Munich, you claim in Munich; if you change planes in another Schengen country, the stamp belongs to that last airport.

## 2. Before check-in: the customs stamp

The checked-bag route has a fixed order: check in and get the boarding pass, but **do not drop the bag**. Global Blue's notice puts it bluntly — get the label for the luggage, but do not drop it. Customs has to match the form to the goods, and once the suitcase is on the belt you will not open it again.

The customs office (Zoll/Customs) sits beside the immigration queue: on its right as you walk towards it, which means **on your left once you are standing facing the immigration desks**. Same spot, different turn. Look for the Zoll sign and a short queue of people holding forms and passports.

The author's purchases were low in value, customs did not ask for the case to be opened, and the stamp took a moment. That is not a guarantee: officers may ask to see the goods, so do not bury them at the bottom of the suitcase and do not break the packaging.

With the stamp in place, hand the Global Blue form to the Global Blue desk on the right of the customs office, then turn left, and left again, following the signs. Many people stop too early: they assume the stamp finished the job, but an unstamped hand-in means no money.

Global Blue is not the only form in circulation. A green Planet Tax Free drop box hangs beside the counter, and its front lists what the envelope must contain: a customs stamp, your full name, address and passport number, the card number, and the original receipt. Other operators work the same way, with a different counter taking the form.

![A green Planet Tax Free drop box mounted on the wall: a slot at the top, the Tax Free planet and Premier tax free logos, and a checklist for customs approval, name and passport number, card number and original receipt](./images/1.webp)

Global Blue's own notice reduces the route to three steps: check in and take the bag label but do not drop the bag; take the labelled luggage to the customs desk right behind the counter; then come back for the refund once the form is stamped.

![A Global Blue notice on the counter headed "How to get your VAT Refund", listing check-in, customs validation and the refund, and warning not to drop the labelled luggage](./images/2.webp)

## 3. After passport control: the customs office at H30

Hand luggage takes the other route. "Leaving" here happens twice: first security and bag check, then passport control. Plenty of travellers assume the paperwork is over after security and only remember the refund at the gate, which means walking all the way back.

The correct sequence: clear passport control, head for gate H30, and just before the gate there is a short corridor on the right whose far end is the Zoll/Customs office. The sign is not large, so look for the blue Zoll panel and turn in.

The author's hand-luggage goods were again not opened, for the same reason — low unit values. With the stamp done, the Global Blue form goes to the counter near gate H30; forms from other companies go to the Reise Bank next door.

The company's notice is more precise about the geography: the customs office for hand luggage is after passport control, inside or opposite **gate H28**; the Global Blue office is at **gate H30**; Planet Tax Free and Reise Bank are also in the H30 area. H28 and H30 are neighbouring gates, so either sign will get you there.

![A Global Blue notice giving locations: the hand-luggage customs office after passport control inside or opposite gate H28, the Global Blue office at gate H30, and Planet Tax Free with Reise Bank at H30, with refunds to card or digital wallet](./images/3.webp)

## 4. Global Blue, Planet and Reise Bank

Munich's refund desks are organised by company, so which counter you visit depends on the logo printed on your form. Each payout also has its own trade-off: cash is immediate but the currency and rate may not suit you, while a card or Alipay refund arrives more slowly but usually keeps more of the amount.

| Company | Where to hand in the form | Typical payout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Global Blue | Beside the customs office (checked bags) or near gate H30 (hand luggage) | Card or digital wallet, as stated on the notice |
| Planet Tax Free | Reise Bank counter in the H30 area | Cash, Alipay or card, chosen at the counter |
| Other operators (e.g. Easy Tax Free) | Reise Bank counter in the H30 area | As above, registered at the counter |

Reise Bank is a German currency-exchange chain; at the airport it is a half-open counter with a yellow Change roundel on the glass. Cash refunds are handled here, and the counter usually holds only a few currencies, so ask which one you can have.

Fill in everything on the form — card number, Alipay account, signature — because a missing field means queueing again.

Anything to be posted goes into the mailbox; the author used the one on the left, close to the counter, and photographed the form first.

## 5. Timing and what to bring

Get the order right and the time works out. With checked bags: check in for the boarding pass, get the customs stamp, return to drop the bag, clear security and passport control, then go to the gate. With hand luggage: check in and drop the bag, clear security and passport control, get the stamp near H28/H30, hand in the form and post anything required. In both cases arrive **three hours** before an international flight.

The checklist: passport, boarding pass, completed form, receipts, sealed goods, and a credit card or an active Alipay account to receive the money. The goods and receipts must match, and the name, passport number and date of leaving the EU cannot be left blank.

Four mistakes worth avoiding: dropping the bag before the stamp; using or unsealing the goods; getting the stamp but never handing the form in; and assuming the refund must be claimed where you bought the goods, when it belongs to your last departure point from the EU.

How long the money takes depends on the company and the payout method — usually a few weeks, so keep the receipt and reference until it lands. Counter positions and company rules change, so check the Munich Airport website, the customs authority and the refund company on the day.
