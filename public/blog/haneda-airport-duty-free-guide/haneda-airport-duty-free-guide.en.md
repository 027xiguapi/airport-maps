Haneda does not have a vast duty-free hall, but it has enough for the last round of shopping before boarding: cosmetics, Japanese whisky, sake, tobacco and sweets, mostly along one stretch of Terminal 3 after security, with a general shop in Terminal 2. What trips people up is rarely the choice of product — it is the opening hours, which terminal the shop is in, and whether the liquids can travel with them onto the next flight.

This guide takes the three shops one at a time, then covers duty-free versus tax-free, ANA vouchers and pre-order, and finally whether to buy at the airport or back in the city. For sweets and souvenirs specifically, see the [companion Haneda airport guide](/blog/haneda-airport-guide).

## 1. Duty-free and tax-free are not the same thing

What you buy at Haneda is **duty-free**: the goods are sold after security, without Japanese consumption tax and without customs duty on imported goods, so imported cosmetics and spirits usually cost less than in town.

What you buy in a city drugstore or department store is **tax-free**: consumption tax only, and with conditions — a minimum of ¥5,000 before tax at one shop on one day, a separate ceiling for consumables such as cosmetics and food, goods sealed in a bag and, in principle, taken out of Japan within 30 days, with a handling fee at some refund counters.

The practical difference in one line: **the airport removes consumption tax and customs duty; the city removes only consumption tax, with paperwork — but you can earn points, try the product and open it straight away.** Japan has decided to move city tax-free shopping to a pay-first, refund-on-departure system (currently scheduled from November 2026); check the National Tax Agency's announcements for the timing and the procedure.

## 2. Terminal 3: cosmetics by gate 109

The main cosmetics shop at T3 is **TIAT DUTY FREE SHOP SOUTH COSMETIC**, in front of **gate 109**, open **06:00–24:30**. It is run by ANA Trading Duty Free and advertises itself as a duty-free shop where you can earn miles — a sensible place to leave the last skincare purchase until just before boarding.

- **Main brands:** ACCEiNE, ADDICTION, CLARINS, Clé de Peau Beauté, COVERMARK, DECORTÉ, Estée Lauder, ELIXIR, FAS, HABA, Kanebo, LUNASOL, ORBIS, POLA, ReFa, SENSAI, SK-II, SHISEIDO, SUQQU, THE GINZA, THREE, TWANY and more, plus fragrance.

![The TIAT DUTY FREE SHOP SOUTH COSMETIC storefront at T3, with SHISEIDO, POLA and SENSAI counters in a row](./images/1.webp)

The shelves are grouped by brand, Japanese and international labels side by side, and the Japanese lines tend to carry a fuller range of shades than city counters. If you want to colour-match or ask for advice, allow twenty minutes or more: the gate is close, but the tills queue at peak times.

![Terminal 3 floor plan: the COSMETIC shop sits in front of gate 109](./images/2.webp)

## 3. Terminal 3: liquor and tobacco, also by gate 109

The same area holds **TIAT DUTY FREE SHOP SOUTH LIQUOR & TOBACCO**, also open **06:00–24:30**. This is the most concentrated place to buy alcohol at Haneda, with regular shelves for Japanese whisky, Scotch, cognac and sake.

- **Japanese whisky:** NIKKA, SUNTORY and others
- **Whisky:** Ballantine's, Chivas Regal, Royal Salute and others
- **Brandy:** MARTELL, Rémy Martin, SUNTORY and others
- **Japanese sake:** Dassai, Gekkeikan, Hakkaisan and others
- Also food, Japanese shochu, cigarettes and heated tobacco

![The T3 liquor and tobacco duty-free shop, with Japanese whisky and cigarettes at the entrance](./images/3.webp)

Think about your next flight before you buy. If you are connecting, see the last section; if you are flying straight home, buy within your destination's allowance. Sake and whisky are liquids: bought before boarding and carried on, they do not count against checked baggage, but you carry them the whole way.

## 4. Terminal 2: TIAT DUTY FREE SOUVENIR

The general duty-free shop at T2 is **TIAT DUTY FREE SOUVENIR**, run by ANA Trading Duty Free, selling liquor, tobacco and food, from bestsellers to buyer-recommended picks. Its hours come in three blocks, with two closed periods in between:

- **06:25–12:50**
- **14:10–15:40**
- **16:35–25:05** (25:05 is 01:05 the next morning)

The split timetable means there is a gap around midday and another in the afternoon, which matters for early and late flights: if your flight falls in 12:50–14:10 or 15:40–16:35, the shop is shut. It sits in T2's international departure area — follow the duty-free signs once you are through immigration.

## 5. ANA vouchers and pre-order pickup

All three shops accept **ANA electronic vouchers, ANA travel vouchers, and ANA in-flight sales and airport shop coupons**. If you are holding any of these, ask at the till whether they can be used, whether change is given and whether they are restricted to certain goods — the rules differ by voucher type, and the shop's notice is definitive.

ANA also runs a duty-free pre-order site: order before you fly and collect in store with a QR code or reservation number. It suits bottles, sets and popular shades you have already decided on, saving time at the shelf and reducing the chance of a sold-out item. Cut-off times, pickup points and which products can be reserved are set by the pre-order site; you still show your passport and boarding pass when you collect.

## 6. Connections, liquids and airport versus city

**A connection is the big variable.** Duty-free liquid bought at Haneda is fine on the flight you board there, but if you transfer at a third airport, the next security check still applies the 100 ml rule. Some airports allow it only in a sealed tamper-evident bag (STEB) with the receipt inside. The safe routine: ask the staff to seal the bag and keep the receipt, confirm in advance whether your transfer airport accepts it, and if you are unsure, buy the bottle at the last airport instead.

**Worth buying at Haneda:** Japanese whisky and airport-only sets, sake such as Dassai, imported cosmetics and fragrance (no consumption tax and no customs duty, usually cheaper than a city tax-free purchase), cigarettes and heated tobacco. **Better bought in the city:** skincare you want to try first, drugstore goods and daily items, and sweets and souvenirs available everywhere — you can earn points, compare prices, and avoid carrying it all.

Then there is the border on the way home. Chinese customs gives resident travellers a duty-free allowance for goods bought abroad (generally ¥5,000, with separate limits for alcohol and tobacco); anything above that must be declared and taxed. Work out the allowance before you shop — it is quicker than comparing bottles at the shelf.
