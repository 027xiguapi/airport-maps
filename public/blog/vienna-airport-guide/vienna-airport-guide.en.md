Vienna International Airport (airport code **VIE**) sits about 18 km from the city centre and is Austria's largest international hub — and one of the most important transfer points between central and eastern Europe. The airport is only 16–25 minutes from town, but the express train, the S-Bahn, the buses and taxis all trade speed against price, and the check-in halls and security entrances are split by terminal and destination. Knowing which building and which entrance are yours saves real time.

This guide covers **terminals and gate areas**, **transport into the city**, **the departure process** and **airport facilities** in four parts.

## 1. Terminals and gate areas

Vienna Airport has three terminals in use — **Terminal 1, Terminal 1A and Terminal 3** — lined up under one roof, so you can walk between them. The SKYLINK extension of Terminal 3 opened in 2012 and can take aircraft as large as the Airbus A380; it is the main building for long-haul flights.

![Map of Vienna Airport: Terminals 1, 1A and 3, the B, C, D, F and G gate areas, car parks P3 and P4 and the Office Park buildings](./images/1.webp)

The airport is run by Flughafen Wien AG, with passenger services, baggage handling and ground handling shared between Vienna Airport, Swissport, Austrian Airlines and others. Check-in desks are allocated by airline, so confirm **which terminal** your flight uses — currently Terminal 1, Terminal 1A or Terminal 3. Older airport material still uses the name "Terminal 2" for a location, but it no longer handles check-in.

### Five gate areas: B, C, D, F and G

Gates are grouped into five piers, and the group decides which security entrance you use and whether you pass through passport control. Pier D was formerly called Hall A and is known as the east pier; pier C is the west pier.

| Pier | Gates | Connection to the main building | Flights |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B (Terminal 1 side) | B22–B43 | Bus | European Schengen flights |
| C (west pier, Terminal 1 side) | C31–C42, of which C35–C41 are transfer gates | Airbridge | Mostly Schengen, some international |
| C (west pier, Terminal 1 side) | C71–C75 | Bus, reached via gate C31 | Schengen only |
| D (east pier, formerly Hall A) | D21–D29 | Airbridge | International flights |
| D (east pier, formerly Hall A) | D31–D37, D61–D70 | Bus | International flights |
| F, G (Terminal 3) | F and G gates | Airbridge | Schengen (F) and non-Schengen (G) |

Airbridge gates walk straight onto the aircraft; gates marked as bus gates mean a trip downstairs and a shuttle ride, which belongs in your boarding time. C71–C75 is a separate bus-gate group reached through gate C31, so allow extra walking and waiting time if you are departing from there.

### International transfers: change planes without entering Austria

If Vienna is only a stopover, **international transfers do not require entering Austria**. Arriving at pier C on an international flight, follow the signs down to the **Transitzone** on the ground floor and take the shuttle bus to pier D for your onward international flight. Arriving at pier D and continuing on another international flight, you make the same transfer inside pier D using the Transitzone and the shuttle bus. At pier D the transit area and the passport control area sit at the entrances to the halls and at the airbridge and bus gates — follow the "Transfer" signs.

## 2. Getting into the city and back

There are five ways into Vienna from the airport: the **City Airport Train** is quickest, the **airport buses** cover the city centre and the western station, the **S7 S-Bahn and Railjet trains** give the best value, **taxis** suit late arrivals and heavy bags, and **car hire** suits a road trip that starts in Vienna. The routes, times and fares below are as published by the airport and the operators; check the operators' sites on the day you travel.

### 1. City Airport Train (CAT) — fastest

The City Airport Train runs non-stop from the airport to **Wien Mitte (Landstraße)** in the city centre in **16 minutes**, which makes it the least stressful way in — road traffic simply is not a factor.

- **Hours:** 05:37–23:29 (city centre → airport); 06:09–23:39 (airport → city centre), roughly every 30 minutes.
- **Stops:** airport ↔ Wien Mitte, about a **10-minute** walk from St. Stephen's Cathedral.
- **Fare:** €14.90 one way, with cheaper return tickets. Vienna's city transport tickets and the Vienna City Card are **not** valid on the CAT; you need a separate ticket.
- **Tickets:** the arrivals hall has a dedicated CAT counter where you can buy tickets and ask about any CAT service.
- **City check-in for baggage:** at the CAT terminal in the city centre you can check in and collect a boarding pass, at staffed desks or self-service kiosks, from 24 hours until 75 minutes before departure.

### 2. Airport buses — widest coverage

The airport buses (Vienna Airport Lines / Postbus) link the airport to Vienna's main transport hubs, and the three city routes between them cover every metro line. Services towards Wien Westbahnhof and Wien Hauptbahnhof run **every 30 minutes**; the airport ↔ Morzinplatz/Schwedenplatz route runs **around the clock** and takes just **20 minutes**.

- **Fare:** about €8 one way, bought on board or at the counter.

### 4. S-Bahn and ÖBB trains — best value

To travel at the price of a city ticket, the **S7** S-Bahn is the answer: about **25 minutes** from the airport to Wien Mitte, at roughly a third of the CAT fare, and the ticket then covers the metro and trams in the city.

ÖBB's Railjet trains connect the airport to places much further out: direct to Wien Hauptbahnhof (15 minutes), St. Pölten, Linz, Salzburg, Innsbruck, Graz and Klagenfurt, with cross-border services to Brno, Praha, Győr and Budapest. At Wien Hauptbahnhof (Vienna Central Station) you can change for many more destinations.

- **Fare:** about €5 one way on the S7 (the VOR transport association prices by zone, and the airport lies outside Vienna's core zone — if you already hold a city ticket, you only pay the airport-section supplement).
- **Tickets:** the ÖBB counter is in the airport arrivals hall, a short walk away, and tickets are also sold at ÖBB machines and in the ÖBB app.

## 3. Departure: check-in, bag drop, security

The standard sequence is **check in → drop your bags → security → (non-Schengen flights) passport control → board**. Arrive 2 hours before a Schengen departure and 3 hours before a non-Schengen or long-haul flight; add half an hour in peak season and around public holidays.

### 1. Check-in

![Vienna Airport check-in: an agent helping passengers at a check-in counter](./images/7.webp)

- **Self-service check-in:** use a credit card or a Miles & More card, your name, your booking reference, or simply scan your passport to get a boarding pass from the self-service machines. If you have bags to check, the machine tells you which bag-drop counter to use.
- **At the counter:** depending on your flight, check in and collect your boarding pass at Terminal 1, Terminal 1A or Terminal 3.
- **Online check-in:** most airlines offer it on their website or app, which saves queueing at the airport; print your boarding pass there. If you are travelling with bags to check, ask at the relevant counter first.
- **Check-in at Wien Mitte:** some airlines offer check-in at the City Airport Train terminal at Wien Mitte, where you show your passport and receive your boarding pass. For the current list of airlines, see cityairporttrain.com or call +43 1 25250.

### 2. Self bag drop

Vienna Airport's self bag drop compresses the handover into four steps: **scan your boarding pass → attach the tag to your bag → keep your claim tag → go on to security.**

![A passenger being helped at the self bag drop belt at Vienna Airport](./images/8.webp)

![The four steps of self bag drop at Vienna Airport: scan your boarding pass, attach the tag to your bag, keep your claim tag, then continue to step 2](./images/9.webp)

At the self-service belts beside the check-in islands, scan your boarding pass to confirm the flight, fix the printed tag to your suitcase handle, put the bag on the belt, and keep the claim tag — if the bag goes astray, it is the only record you have.

### 3. Baggage notes

- For **overweight baggage or pets**, use the **oversized baggage counters at Terminal 1 or Terminal 3**.
- Put your name and address on the bag and remove old tags — old barcodes confuse the sorting system and make a misrouted bag more likely.
- Carry valuables, important documents and medication with you rather than checking them.
- Secure the bag with a strap or padlock if needed, or use the airport's baggage wrapping service (see the facilities section below).

### 4. Security and passport control

Security entrances are split by gate group, and the wrong one costs you a long walk:

- **Security entrance 2:** for departures from gates B, C and D.
- **Security entrance 3:** for departures from gates F and G.

Past security there are shops and restaurants in the departure area; friends and family seeing you off cannot pass through with you.

- For **Schengen countries**, use gates **B, C and F**.
- For **non-Schengen countries**, use gates **D and G**, and you must pass through **passport control** before continuing to the gate.

## 4. Airport facilities and practical information

### Enquiries and baggage services

There are **three information desks**: two on the departures levels of Terminals 1 and 3, and one in the Terminal 3 arrivals hall. Baggage and facility questions can all be answered here.

The **baggage service centres** in the check-in areas of Terminals 1 and 3 are open **00:00–24:00** and offer:

| Service | Price | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Left luggage | €4 per item per day; oversized items €8 per item per day | Items can be stored for up to 6 months |
| Baggage wrapping | €12 per item | Your bag is sealed in protective film against damage, damp and theft; available in the baggage areas of Terminals 1 and 3 |
| Baggage delivery | from €15 for up to 5 items, €3 for each extra item | Book at least 72 hours ahead by emailing service@viennaairport.com, or book directly in the airport's online shop |
| Safe deposit boxes | €6 per day | For documents, keys and similar; weapons, explosives, flammable or other dangerous goods, and perishable or strong-smelling items may not be stored |
| Wardrobe | €2 per set per day | One set is a jacket or coat, shoes, jewellery, gloves and a scarf |

### Medical centre, prayer room and lost property

- **Medical centre:** Terminal 1, level 1 corridor, opposite the pharmacy, open **07:00–19:00**. Vaccinations include yellow fever, typhoid, rabies, hepatitis A and B (also for children), malaria, tick-borne encephalitis, influenza, the combined diphtheria/polio/tetanus/pertussis vaccine, meningococcal disease and Japanese encephalitis — call ahead to check stock.
- **Prayer room:** there is a prayer room in the terminal; ask at an information desk for its location.
- **Lost property:** the lost property office on level 0 of Terminal 1, or the information desk in the Terminal 3 arrivals hall.
- **Returning lost items by post:** use the delivery and packing service of Mail Boxes Etc. in A-2320 Schwechat, or any courier you prefer; the contact address is mbe0045@mbe.at. Items held longer than 8 days incur a storage charge, and **payment must clear before the item is sent**: for card payments the invoice and payment link arrive by email; for bank transfers, put the invoice number in the payment reference field, otherwise your payment cannot be identified.

### Customs and duty-free allowances

Travellers entering Austria from outside the EU may bring in the following within the duty-free allowances. **Travellers on the same trip cannot pool their allowances** — a rule that is often missed:

![Austrian customs duty-free allowances: goods worth up to €430, 200 cigarettes or 50 cigars or 100 cigarillos or 250 g of tobacco, plus 4 litres of wine and other alcohol allowances](./images/10.webp)

| Category | Duty-free allowance |
| --- | --- |
| Total value of goods | up to **€430** (air travellers only; €300 for arrivals by land) |
| Cigarettes | 200 |
| or cigars | 50 |
| or cigarillos | 100 |
| or tobacco | 250 g |
| or a combination of the above | — |
| plus wine | 4 L |
| plus spirits over 22% alcohol by volume | 1 L |
| or spirits or aperitifs under 22% | 2 L |
| or sparkling wine or liqueurs | 2 L |
| or a combination of the above | — |

- **Age limits:** passengers under 15 have an allowance of €150; the alcohol and tobacco allowances apply from age 17.
- **Cash declarations:** carrying more than **€10,000** in cash or other means of payment (travellers' cheques, gold bars, gold coins and the like) into or out of the EU must be declared to customs; forms are available at the customs counter or a customs office.
- **Export refund stamps:** the customs office for exports is on arrivals level 0.

The airport's currency exchange desks are run by **Interchange**, with branches at the gates, in the arrivals hall and in the terminals, generally open from 5 a.m. into the late evening; ATMs are spread even more widely, from the arrivals hall to the gate piers.

ATMs are at the arrivals hall, gates C/D/F/G, the Plaza, car park 8, Terminals 1, 2 and 3 public areas, Office Park 1, Office Park 3 and the VIP/GAC area. The ATMs in the arrivals hall, car parks, terminals and office buildings are open to everyone; those at the gates and the Plaza are for passengers only.

### Wi-Fi, power and SIM cards

- **Wi-Fi:** covers the whole terminal, so you can check flights and message home while you wait.
- **Power and work:** gates F and G have dedicated work spaces where you can plug in and use a laptop.
- **SIM cards and repairs:** Mr. Mobile offers phone, tablet and laptop repairs, loan handsets and prepaid SIM cards.
