As France's largest international airport and one of Europe's key aviation hubs, **Paris Charles de Gaulle (CDG)** is where most visitors first meet the city. It lies at Roissy, about 25 km north-east of Paris, covers roughly 30 square kilometres and has **210 gates** — the most of any airport in the world.

This guide goes through the **terminal layout**, **transport inside the airport**, **left luggage**, **banks and currency exchange**, **tax refunds**, **rest areas and lounges**, **Wi-Fi and charging**, **SIM cards**, and **accessibility and family facilities** — the services worth knowing about before you land.

## 1. Terminals and their floors

### Three terminals, and seven sub-terminals inside T2

CDG has **three terminals: 1, 2 and 3**. **T2 is the main one**, split into **seven blocks — T2A, T2B, T2C, T2D, T2E, T2F and T2G** — arranged symmetrically along the south side of the airport, and it also includes pier K and the two satellites L and M; it is used mainly by **Air France and its SkyTeam partners**. **T3 is in the north**, small and simple, and handles only some low-cost carriers.

![Map of Paris CDG: Terminals 1, 3 and the T2 sub-terminals with the car parks, the CDGVAL line and the shuttle buses, and a legend for trains, bus stations, parking, taxis and hotels](./images/1.webp)

T1 stands to the north-west: a circular, flying-saucer-shaped building, the other main terminal, used chiefly by members of the **Star Alliance and oneworld** alliances.

### What each floor of T1 does

T1 spreads check-in, departures, arrivals and dining across nine levels, so picking the right floor saves a lot of walking:

| Level | Purpose | What is there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Transport level | CDGVAL train, restaurants, a few check-in desks |
| 2 | Check-in level | Check-in desks, taxi drop-off, bus station |
| 3 | Departures | Security, border control, duty-free shops |
| 4 | Arrivals | Baggage carousels |
| 5–7 | Parking | Car parks |
| 8 | Dining | Restaurants, offices |
| 9 | Observation | Viewing deck |

![Original reference: which facilities sit on each level of T1 at Paris CDG](./images/2.webp)

### Which airlines use which terminal

The split between T1, T2 and T3 is substantial, and inside T2 you also need the sub-terminal (Air China Eastern is at T2E, for example, and Hainan Airlines at T2A). Always check the terminal code on your boarding pass:

| Terminal | Main airlines (from the original material) |
| --- | --- |
| T1 | Air China, EVA Air, China Airlines, United, Lufthansa, ANA, Eurowings, Qatar Airways, Turkish Airlines, Singapore Airlines, SWISS, SAS, Norwegian, LOT, Aegean |
| T2 | China Eastern (T2E), China Southern (T2E), Hainan Airlines (T2A), Cathay Pacific (T2A), Air France (T2E, T2F), British Airways (T2A), American Airlines (T2A), Delta (T2E), Air Canada (T2A), Japan Airlines (T2E), Korean Air (T2E), Emirates (T2C), Aeroflot (T2C), Finnair (T2D), Alitalia (T2F), KLM (T2F), Ukraine International (T2D), easyJet (T2D) |
| T3 | Mainly Spanish routes such as Vueling, plus charter and seasonal Mediterranean services |

![Original reference: the airline-to-terminal table for Paris CDG (T1, the T2 sub-terminals and T3)](./images/3.webp)

Terminal assignments change (Germanwings and Alitalia in the original list have since ceased or merged), so confirm with your airline and the airport for the day you travel.

## 2. Getting around inside the airport: CDGVAL and shuttle buses

CDG's terminals are far apart, and crossing the airport sometimes needs a vehicle. There are two: the **free CDGVAL shuttle train** (also called RoissyVal) and the **Navette shuttle buses**, both running between T1, T2, T3 and the car parks.

![The CDGVAL entrance at Paris CDG, signed "CDGVAL Airport Shuttle 戴高乐机场穿梭轻轨", with directions overhead to Terminals 2 and 3, the railway station and the bus station](./images/4.webp)

Although the terminals are a fair distance apart the layout is logical, and most signs now carry Chinese, so following them is enough. Allow 20–30 minutes when changing terminals — a CDGVAL hop takes minutes, but waiting and walking do not.

## 3. Left luggage

If you want to see Paris first, or have a long connection, leave your bags behind:

- **Where:** at the **TGV station concourse of Terminal 2**, near the Sheraton hotel;
- **Security:** X-ray screening and permanent video surveillance;
- **Pricing:** flexible, by the hour or by the day;
- **Extras:** storage for special or valuable items, pet-friendly facilities, plus baggage wrapping and hotel delivery;
- **Languages:** multilingual staff.

![The orange "Consignes à bagages / Left Luggage" wall at Paris CDG, in the Terminal 2 interchange area](./images/5.webp)

## 4. Banks and currency exchange

- **Every terminal has currency exchange desks** (Global Exchange and others) dealing in euros and foreign currency; they sit in both the public and the customs areas, near the check-in halls and the gates.
- **ATMs** are spread through the terminals for cash withdrawals and everyday transactions — in the arrivals hall and near the gates.

![An ATM in the CDG arrivals area, beside a "Transports / Ground Transport" information pillar](./images/6.webp)

- Note that **most banks charge for overseas withdrawals**, so check your own bank's fees and limits before you travel. Exchange desks at the airport rarely beat the city on rate, so change only what you need for transport and emergencies.

![Euros withdrawn from a CDG ATM — the machines take UnionPay, Visa and Mastercard](./images/7.webp)

## 5. Tax refunds (détaxe)

Non-EU residents (aged 16 or over, staying in France or the EU for less than six months) can reclaim VAT on purchases of **€100.01 or more, including VAT, in the same shop on the same day**. Bring your **passport, all the goods, the tax-free form and the receipt**; the goods must be unused and in their original packaging.

The validation points at CDG:

| Terminal | Where to claim |
| --- | --- |
| T1 | One level below the CDGVAL level |
| T2A / T2B / T2C / T2D | Departure level, near gate 6 |
| T2E | Departure level, near gate 4 |
| T2F / T3 | Arrival level |

![The Travelex tax-refund desk at Paris CDG, with a traveller holding a passport and Global Blue forms](./images/8.webp)

The process, and the parts people get wrong:

1. Ask for the tax-free form in the shop (bring your passport), then scan its barcode at a **PABLO self-service terminal** — a green "OK Bordereau validé" screen is the customs stamp;
2. **Do this before you check in or check your bags**, because customs may want to see the unused goods;
3. French forms can only be validated at the machines; **goods bought in other EU countries must be stamped at a customs counter**;
4. Validation is valid for **three months plus the current month** from the purchase date, and refunds normally arrive 6–8 weeks after validation.

![The screen of a PABLO tax-refund terminal at Paris CDG (DÉTAXE - TAX REFUND - 退税), listing what you must carry — goods, passport, travel ticket — and that you must leave the EU afterwards](./images/9.webp)

## 6. Rest areas and lounges

- **Free rest areas:** the airport provides free seating areas to relax before a flight or during a connection; if you have no hotel or want to save money, you can also spend the night there. They are usually near the furthest gates — follow the signs.
- **Paid rest area:** inside the free area there is a separate paid zone where you can rest and take a shower.
- **Lounges:** business- and first-class passengers and airline elite members can use the lounges; economy passengers can usually pay to enter.
- **Work zones:** shared workspaces in the terminals, with desks, power sockets and decent lighting.
- **Plane spotting:** some rest areas look straight out over the runway.
- **Smoking:** all terminals have designated smoking areas.

## 7. Wi-Fi, charging and SIM cards

- **Free Wi-Fi:** covers every terminal; join the **"WIFI-AIRPORT"** network and you are connected.
- **Charging:** look for the **ChargeBox** stations, free to use in the main terminals' post-security departure halls (Terminal 1; T2D; the L and M halls of T2E; Terminal 3).

![Tiered seating in a Paris CDG work zone, with small tables and power sockets](./images/12.webp)

- **SIM cards:** every terminal has **Relay, Fnac and Orange** counters selling local SIM cards and accessories, with multilingual staff and in-store activation — buy it and plug it in. The common tourist option is Orange Holiday (for example 100 GB / 5G at about €49.99), available over the counter.

![The Orange tourist SIM counter at Paris CDG, advertising "Tourist SIM card 100GB 5G" at €49.99 and ready in 2 minutes](./images/13.webp)

Counters and opening hours:

## 8. Accessibility and family facilities

**Assistance (PWD):**

- The terminals provide assistance for passengers with disabilities — book with your airline or travel agent **at least 48 hours** before departure;
- Meeting points sit inside and outside the terminals, with self-service wheelchairs and pushchairs;
- Modern, accessible changing rooms cover families with children and travellers with disabilities;
- T2E and T2F each have a **dog area**, giving assistance dogs a comfortable, safe space.

![The Paris Aéroport "ASSISTANCE MOBILITÉ / MOBILITY ASSISTANCE" desk, with staff helping a passenger in a wheelchair](./images/14.webp)

**Family facilities:**

- Free services for travellers with babies or small children;
- Baby-changing areas and toilets designed for young children;
- Some terminals have **baby rooms** with full nursing equipment, including bottle warmers and cots.
