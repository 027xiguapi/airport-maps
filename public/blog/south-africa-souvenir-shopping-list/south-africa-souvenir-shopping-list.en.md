What should you buy before leaving South Africa? Here are eight things picked for being easy to pack, genuinely useful and distinctly local.

## 1. Wood carvings: collect the Big Five

Most carvings come from East Africa, and sandalwood pieces are the prized ones. The author bought a set of Big Five carvings (lion, elephant, rhino, buffalo, leopard) plus an Africa-shaped piece and a giraffe — a set like this makes a good keepsake.

![Sandalwood Big Five carvings, an Africa-shaped piece and a giraffe](./images/1.webp)

**How to choose:** judge weight and grain in the hand — sandalwood feels dense with fine grain. Uneven staining and visible joins usually mean a cheap reproduction.

## 2. Aloe gel: the local staple

South African aloe gel is close to a must-buy, most commonly at 99% purity. Good for after-sun care and everyday moisturising.

![99% aloe gel, often sold in packs of three](./images/2.webp)

## 3. Rooibos tea

Rooibos grows only in South Africa, is naturally caffeine-free, and works hot or iced. It comes plain or flavoured (vanilla, honeybush) and makes an easy gift to split among friends.

![Rooibos and black teas: JOKO, Five Roses, Lipton and others](./images/3.webp)

## 4. Chocolate: go for the nut bars

There is a wide range of nut chocolates, so pick a few flavours.

![Assorted nut chocolates](./images/4.webp)

## 5. Dried mango: buy it at Johannesburg duty-free

Made from South African mangoes in season, with no additives, the mango flavour is intense. The author bought hers in the **duty-free shops at Johannesburg airport** — easy to pick up on the way to the gate.

![Additive-free South African dried mango](./images/5.webp)

## 6. Amarula: the national cream liqueur

Amarula is a cream liqueur made from African marula fruit. The elephant on the label is why Chinese travellers call it "elephant liquor". Also available in the **Johannesburg airport duty-free** area.

![Two bottles of Amarula](./images/6.webp)

## 7. Diamonds: insist on a certificate

The author bought a necklace at a South African diamond workshop for about **42% of the list price**. For natural diamonds that is a fair price.

**Careful:** always ask for a proper grading certificate (GIA, HRD and so on) and check the certificate number against the laser inscription on the stone. Tourist shops at airports and in town usually charge a much bigger premium.

## 8. Cigars

Also bought at the Johannesburg airport duty-free; the author describes them as full-bodied and strong. Note that China's duty-free tobacco allowance on entry is limited — anything above it must be declared.

## Where to buy

| Item | Best place |
| --- | --- |
| Dried mango, Amarula, cigars | Johannesburg airport duty-free, before you fly |
| Aloe gel, rooibos, chocolate | Supermarkets (Woolworths, Pick n Pay) — cheaper and more choice |
| Wood carvings | Craft markets or the airport; check the material |
| Diamonds | A licensed workshop, and always with a certificate |

## Practical notes

- Aloe gel, alcohol and other liquids must go in **checked baggage**.
- Check Chinese import rules for plant and animal products (wood, seeds) before you pack them.
- Duty-free allowances are limited: keep receipts and certificates for valuables such as diamonds and cigars, and declare them on arrival.
