Landing at Suvarnabhumi for the first time, the worry is not knowing which way to walk. This guide breaks the whole international-arrivals route into one picture per step, so you can simply follow it — useful for an older traveller flying alone, or for the family waiting outside.

## The route in one line

**Off the plane → jet bridge or bus → follow the "Arrivals" signs → immigration → baggage claim → Exit → public area**

> Suvarnabhumi's signage is excellent, and **the main signs carry Chinese as well as Thai and English**, so you should not get lost on the way.

## Step by step

### 1. Follow the crowd off the plane

You normally leave through a jet bridge, and there is only one route — just follow everyone else.

![Following the crowd along the jet bridge](./images/1.webp)

### 2. Keep walking and watch for the flight screen

A little further on there is a screen showing your flight.

![The corridor towards the arrivals hall](./images/2.webp)

It shows **your flight number, the city you came from, and your baggage carousel** (number 14 in the photo below). Note your number down.

![Flight screen: ZH307 from Shenzhen, baggage claim 14](./images/3.webp)

### 3. Look for the black "Arrivals" sign

Once you are out of the jet bridge you reach the signs. Look for the **black panel reading "Arrivals" (旅客到达)** — it usually sits at the top of the board — and set off in that direction (about 190 metres).

![The black "Arrivals" panel at the top of the sign, 190 metres away](./images/4.webp)

You pass escalators and travelators on the way; just keep following "Arrivals".

![Continuing along the "Arrivals" route](./images/5.webp)

### 4. Submit the TDAC arrival card before immigration

Thailand now uses the **digital arrival card, TDAC (Thailand Digital Arrival Card)**, which must be completed online **before you reach immigration**, at [tdac.immigration.go.th](https://tdac.immigration.go.th).

There is a large notice board on the walkway. If the traveller does not read English, a younger relative can fill it in on a phone in advance and save a screenshot of the QR code.

![The TDAC notice board](./images/6.webp)

### 5. Turn left at the junction into customs

This large sign marks the junction: "Arrivals" to the left, and Connecting Flights and the Transfers Desk to the right. Passengers heading into the city **turn left here** and go through to the customs area.

![The junction sign: arrivals to the left, transfers to the right](./images/7.webp)

### 6. Immigration: take the foreign passport lane

In the customs area, join the **Foreign Passport** queue. **Photography is not allowed inside** — put your phone away.

![The immigration area, queueing for passport control](./images/8.webp)

Have these ready:

- **Your return ticket** (or an onward itinerary)
- **Your hotel booking**
- **Cash:** Thai immigration can spot-check funds — around 10,000 baht per person, 20,000 baht per family

If your paperwork is in order you are usually asked nothing more. If you do not follow the English, show your phone's translation to the officer.

### 7. Collect your baggage

Past customs is the baggage claim hall. Look up at the signs and find your carousel number.

![The baggage claim area after customs](./images/9.webp)

![Following the numbered directions to the carousels](./images/10.webp)

If you checked a bag, wait at the carousel with your number; **if you have no checked bag, walk straight on towards the exit**.

![Carousel 14: the screen shows your flight's baggage status](./images/11.webp)

### 8. Find your Exit letter and step into the public area

Exits are lettered (A, B, C and so on) and any of them leads out to the same public walkway.

![The Exit signs](./images/12.webp)

Outside is the public area, where the pillar or overhead sign tells you **which gate number you are at** (gate 5 below). It is the easiest place to meet family or friends — or to carry straight on to a taxi or the Airport Rail Link.

![Gate numbers in the public area, where pick-ups meet you](./images/13.webp)

## If you are meeting someone

- Ask the arriving traveller to **note their carousel number and exit letter** — with those two details you can locate them.
- Suvarnabhumi's arrivals level has several exit sections, so **agreeing on "gate 5" beats agreeing on "the exit"**.
- From landing to stepping into the public area normally takes **45–75 minutes**, including immigration and baggage. Allow a little slack when you set off to collect someone.
