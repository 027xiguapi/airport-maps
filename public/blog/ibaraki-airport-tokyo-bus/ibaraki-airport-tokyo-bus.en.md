Ibaraki Airport (IBR) sits in Omitama, Ibaraki Prefecture, about 85 km north-east of Tokyo. There is no railway station: everything moves by road, with one highway coach into Tokyo Station and local buses to Ishioka, Mito and Tsukuba from the bays outside the terminal.

The Tokyo service has changed a lot in recent years. The fare was revised on 30 March 2025, bookings moved to Japan Bus Online with payment in advance, and the 2026 timetable is down to two runs a week. The ¥500 singles and dozen-a-day timetables that still circulate online are long gone; plan around them and you miss the bus. This guide follows the current rules.

## 1. 85 km, 100 minutes

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Route | Ibaraki Airport ⇄ Tokyo Station, Yaesu South Exit |
| Distance and time | about 85 km, about 100 minutes |
| One-way fare | ¥2,200 adult, ¥1,100 child (since 30 March 2025) |
| Payment | cash or transport IC card |
| Boarding in Tokyo | Yaesu South Exit highway bus stop 3; coaches from the airport set down at the Nihonbashi Exit |
| Boarding at the airport | stop 3 outside the terminal |
| Operator and enquiries | Kantetsu (Kanto Railway) Mito office, 029-247-5111, 8:00–19:00 |

The 100 minutes is the airport's own figure: the coach runs via the Joban and Shuto expressways, so it holds in clear traffic and stretches on holidays and in the evening peak. The real value is that it is direct — no changing trains at Ishioka or Tsukuba with luggage, and you step off at Tokyo Station. The terminal is small, and signs lead you straight to the bays.

## 2. The timetable: Mondays and Fridays only

| Direction | Departs | Runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tokyo Station → Ibaraki Airport | 11:20 | Monday, Friday |
| Ibaraki Airport → Tokyo Station | 15:25 | Monday, Friday |

This is the summer timetable, 13 April to 24 October 2026, with two runs a week in each direction. Schedules follow the seasons: the winter and peak versions can differ in dates and times, and the [airport's timetable page](https://www.ibaraki-airport.net/en/access/bus/tokyo/) states the period it covers. Check it before booking — an old screenshot is not a timetable.

Plan backwards from those two runs. Tokyo to the airport leaves at 11:20 and reaches the terminal at 13:00, comfortable for an afternoon flight and useless for a morning one. Airport to Tokyo leaves at 15:25, so you need to be landside by about 14:30. If your date is not a Monday or a Friday, the coach does not exist for you and the local buses plus a train are the fallback. If your dates are still open, pick a day that has a run and work the flight around it.

## 3. Booking: one month ahead on Japan Bus Online

The service is reservation-based in principle. Booking opens one month before travel and closes at 19:00 the day before, and the fare is paid online through [Japan Bus Online](https://japanbusonline.com/). Without a reservation you can queue at the stop on the day and take a spare seat if there is one; seats are not guaranteed, and availability is a question for the staff at the stop.

![The route list on the booking page, with Ibaraki Airport ⇄ Tokyo Station at the top](./images/1.webp)

Check the direction when you book: Tokyo Station and the airport are separate options, and there is no second bus that day to fall back on. When the booking completes you get a reservation number and a confirmation email. Keep the email or a screenshot, because the driver asks for it when you board and when you get off. The driver is Japanese and does not speak Chinese, so the screenshot is the quickest way through. Changes and cancellations go through the operator, Kantetsu, and should be made before you reach the stop.

![The confirmation page, which gives a reservation number and asks you to tell staff when boarding](./images/2.webp)

## 4. From arrivals to the stop in three minutes

After immigration, baggage and the customs declaration, the exit on your left is the closest one to the bus stop; the bays are a short walk away, three minutes at an easy pace.

The Tokyo coach leaves from stop 3, and the bays are separated by destination — read the signs, and arrive fifteen minutes early, since check-in and luggage take time. Staff at the stop handle check-in, carry bags and keep the queue moving: check in, then board in order. On board there is free Wi-Fi, with the instructions in the pocket of the seat in front of you; the afternoon sun is strong, so the curtain is worth closing. The coach sets down at Tokyo Station's Nihonbashi Exit, within walking distance of hotels in Marunouchi and Yaesu; follow the station signs for the Shinkansen or the Yamanote line.

![The free Wi-Fi card posted inside the coach, with the SSID and password](./images/3.webp)

## 5. If you miss the bus

With so few runs, a missed coach means a different route. Local buses reach Ishioka station, Mito and Tsukuba: change at Ishioka for the JR Joban line into Tokyo, or at Tsukuba for the Tsukuba Express. Both add a change and time, and neither is fun with heavy luggage. Taxis and reservation-only shared taxis are the other option, but a long taxi into Tokyo is expensive. Schedules for the Ishioka and Tsukuba buses are on the airport site as well.

![Access diagram for Ibaraki Airport: the Joban line to Ishioka or the Tsukuba Express to Tsukuba, then a bus to the terminal](./images/4.webp)

So the order is: confirm the date, confirm whether there is an 11:20 or a 15:25, then book the flight and the hotel. Once the 100 minutes and the confirmation screenshot are part of the plan, the coach is the easy part of the trip.
