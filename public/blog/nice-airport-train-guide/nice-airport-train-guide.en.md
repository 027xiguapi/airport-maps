![Cover collage for the train route from Nice to the airport: the Nice Saint-Augustin sign, a Nice platform and an airport terminal](./images/0.webp)

Nice is hard to skip on a trip through the south of France. Most visitors arrive by high-speed train from Paris, Lyon or Marseille, stay somewhere near the Promenade des Anglais, spend the days in the old town, Èze or Monaco, and fly out at the end. That last day is where the trouble starts: Nice Côte d'Azur Airport (NCE) sits west of the city, close on a map but awkward with luggage.

There are three usual ways out: the airport bus, the tram and the train. The buses stop at a few points in the city and are cheap, but they use the coast road, which can seize up in summer; the tram is slower but frequent. The train is the quickest and the cheapest of the three — from Nice-Ville, the main station, to **Nice Saint-Augustin**, the stop beside the airport, takes four to six minutes for a single fare of **€4**.

This guide follows the route in the original note: buy a ticket and go through the gates at Nice-Ville, ride four to six minutes, walk from Saint-Augustin to Terminal 1, and finish with the free tram between the two terminals. No taxi, and no need to understand French announcements.

## 1. Why the train

The coastline at Nice curves, the airport was built behind the western beaches, and the railway happens to skim past the northern edge of the airfield. That is how Saint-Augustin, once an ordinary suburban halt, became the airport's de facto station: it is only 300–400 metres from the terminal, and it has been rebuilt in recent years to handle the traffic.

The first advantage is that **the train does not sit in traffic**. The road from the city to the airport queues at rush hour and through the summer season, and buses routinely run ten minutes late. The train has its own track, and four to six minutes means four to six minutes. The second advantage is price: €4 compares well with a taxi and with the dedicated airport bus services.

A third advantage is easy to overlook: **a station is simpler than an airport terminal**. Nice-Ville is the city's main station, with the platforms starting immediately behind the concourse; scan your code, walk to the platform, get on, and the whole thing takes a few minutes. At the other end you leave Saint-Augustin, cross a short path and one road, and you are at the door of Terminal 1. No long queues, and no need to arrive two hours early.

The train does have limits, though. It reaches Saint-Augustin but not Terminal 2; services thin out in the evening; and some platform changes involve a footbridge. Those are covered below.

## 2. Buying a ticket and getting through the gates

Nice-Ville is the city's main station and a photogenic one: pale stone walls, arched windows, a few palm trees outside and a sign reading "Nice Ville". From the centre, take the tram or a bus to the Jean Médecin area and walk ten minutes, or take a taxi to the door if the bags are heavy.

![The facade of Nice-Ville station: pale stone walls, arched windows and palm trees, with a "Nice Ville" sign](./images/1.webp)

There are three ways to buy. The simplest is the ticket machine inside the station: the screen switches to English, you choose Nice Saint-Augustin, pay by card or coins, and the ticket prints. The second is **SNCF Connect**, the French national railway's official app, which issues an e-ticket in French or English. The third is **Omio**, which has a Chinese interface and is easier for anyone unused to French booking systems; prices usually match the official site and are occasionally a little higher.

What you get is an e-ticket, and the QR code on your phone is enough — **there is no need to collect paper**. The entrance at Nice-Ville is a row of automatic gates: hold the code to the reader, the gate opens, and you are through. If it will not read, there is usually a member of staff nearby, and showing the order page works just as well.

![The ticket gates at Nice-Ville: passengers queueing to scan their codes, with a green light above the white gate](./images/2.webp)

Do not hurry down to a platform straight away. Screens in the concourse show "Départs / Departures", listing each train, its destination, departure time and platform (voie). Nice letters its platforms — A, B, C — and the screen shows the letter in a box. **Check the platform before you move**, because trains in the same direction sometimes leave from different tracks.

![A "Départs/Departures" screen in the Nice-Ville concourse: TER services, destinations and platform letters on the right, with a ticket machine below](./images/3.webp)

One detail is easy to miss: **check the direction**. Saint-Augustin is west of Nice, so only trains towards Cannes, Grasse and Marseille call there; trains towards Menton and Ventimiglia run east and never reach the airport. The destination on the screen tells you which is which, and a glance before boarding saves an awkward conversation later.

## 3. Four to six minutes

The hop from Nice to Saint-Augustin is one of the shortest on the coastal line. The train leaves Nice-Ville and runs inland of the shoreline, reaching Saint-Augustin almost at once, with **no intermediate stop** — four to six minutes in total, short enough that you have barely settled before it is time to get off.

![The platforms at Nice-Ville: TER trains under a huge glass-and-steel roof, with passengers waiting](./images/4.webp)

Rolling stock varies. Sometimes it is a single-deck TER, sometimes a double-decker. The double-deckers have a staircase in the vestibule, so with luggage it is easier to stay on the lower level than to haul a case upstairs. Inside there are blue upholstered seats, small tables by the window and luggage racks overhead; **any free seat is yours**, since a second-class ticket carries no reservation and needs none.

![Inside a TER carriage: the staircase of a double-decker, blue seats and a red LED sign scrolling the destination towards Cagnes](./images/5.webp)

For four to six minutes the train runs through the western suburbs. Outside the window come dense apartment blocks, then a spread of rail tracks and low workshop buildings, and finally the airport runway and the roofs of the terminals. When you see aircraft, start getting ready to get off.

Frequency is another reason to like this line. From Nice-Ville the **first train is at 05:33 and the last at 22:42**, and during the day a train runs **roughly every 10 to 20 minutes**, so there is no need to arrive early — the platforms start behind the concourse and ten minutes at the station is plenty. The times that need thought are early morning and late evening. For a flight leaving before 07:00, the 05:33 gets you to Saint-Augustin around 05:40, which is just enough with the walk and check-in; anything earlier means a taxi. Anyone landing after 22:42 should plan on a taxi or the airport bus rather than counting on the last train.

## 4. The walk from Saint-Augustin to Terminal 1

Saint-Augustin is a new station. Green fencing, grey name signs and a large board outside reading "Votre nouvelle gare de Nice Saint-Augustin" all point to a recent rebuild. On arrival, do not hunt for an exit immediately: walk with the flow to the end of the platform and look up for "Sortie / Exit" and the aircraft symbol.

![The green fence and grey name sign of Nice Saint-Augustin station](./images/6.webp)

Some platforms require a footbridge to leave. The bridge has stairs and a lift, and with a large case the lift is worth the wait. From the bridge you can see the whole station: a handful of tracks, the overhead wires, and the airport buildings in the distance, which makes the direction obvious.

![The view from the Saint-Augustin platform: tracks, overhead lines and airport buildings beyond, with a footbridge at the end of the platform](./images/7.webp)

Outside, the signs are clear. A dark grey panel reads "Av. Me Maurice Slama" and "Sortie / Exit", with **Bus**, **Tramway** and **Aéroport** beneath it and an arrow pointing ahead. Follow the aircraft symbol and you are heading for the terminal.

![A dark grey sign outside Saint-Augustin station: Av. Me Maurice Slama, Sortie/Exit, and icons for bus, tram, airport, toilets and parking](./images/8.webp)

Next comes a covered walkway with a pale permeable surface, low shrubs and wire fencing on either side. It is not long, and at the end there is a flight of steps and a glass lift tower; to reach Terminal 1 you go down here and pass beneath the footbridge to pick up the pavement towards the airport.

![The covered walkway outside Saint-Augustin station, with travellers pulling a suitcase along the pale paving](./images/9.webp)

The steps and the lift tower are the only part of the walk that needs a decision. Anyone with heavy luggage, a pushchair or limited mobility should take the lift; it is small, and in high season you may wait for one or two trips. The stairs are not steep, but a heavy case makes them hard work.

![The steps beside the footbridge and the glass lift tower, with apartment blocks and the railway embankment behind](./images/10.webp)

Passing the station building is a good moment to fix the direction in your mind: the new entrance is a row of white steel canopies, the name board reads "Gare de Nice Saint-Augustin", and there are usually passengers waiting outside. This is also where you board on the way back — walk over from the airport, and seeing that sign means you have arrived.

![The entrance to the new Saint-Augustin station: passengers waiting under white steel canopies, with the station name on the board](./images/11.webp)

Outside stands a dark blue board: "Votre nouvelle gare de Nice Saint-Augustin", with "Entrée" and an arrow pointing right, and along the bottom the logos of the French state, SNCF Gares & Connexions, the Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur region and the city of Nice. An airport luggage trolley is often parked in front of it, which sums up the relationship between the two.

![A dark blue board outside Saint-Augustin station reading "Votre nouvelle gare de Nice Saint-Augustin" and "Entrée", with an airport luggage trolley in front](./images/12.webp)

A little further on comes the only road crossing of the walk. There is a signal and a zebra crossing, and on the far side the airport's drop-off lanes and the glass frontage of Terminal 1. **From the station exit to the terminal door is about 300–400 metres, or 8 to 12 minutes on foot**; with big cases, a slower pace or a stop for photographs, allow fifteen.

![The road crossing on the way from Saint-Augustin to the airport: zebra crossing, traffic lights and traffic, with the terminal's curved roof opposite](./images/13.webp)

Across the road is the Terminal 1 drop-off area. "Terminal 1" is written above the glass wall, taxis and airport buses stop outside, and the doors open into the check-in hall. By this point, counting from Nice-Ville, barely half an hour has passed.

![The drop-off lane at Nice airport Terminal 1: "Terminal 1" and "Bienvenue/Welcome" above the glass frontage, with cars parked at the kerb](./images/14.webp)

There is one practical problem with this walk: **there is very little shade**. Apart from the canopy outside the station and the short stretch under the footbridge, most of it is in full sun. The Nice summer sun is strong, so a hat and sunscreen matter more than anything else at midday, and a bottle of water is worth carrying, especially with a case.

On the way back, simply reverse it: leave Terminal 1 arrivals, walk along the drop-off lane to the zebra crossing, cross, look for the white canopies of "Gare de Nice Saint-Augustin", then take the stairs or the lift up to the platform. Allow ten to fifteen minutes for the walk in that direction too, since baggage and wayfinding eat into the time; if your ticket names a specific train, put the walk before the departure time rather than after it.

## 5. The free tram from Terminal 1 to Terminal 2

If your flight leaves from Terminal 2, reaching Terminal 1 is not the end of it. Terminal 2 is on the other side of the airport, walking would mean a long detour, and the right move is the free airport tram outside Terminal 1: **line 2** (some sources label it line B). It takes two to three minutes, runs **about every two minutes**, and costs nothing.

![A pillar sign outside Terminal 1: a green arrow for "Quai en face/Opposite Platform", the tram 2/B symbol and "Terminal 2 — Liaison gratuite/Free connection"](./images/15.webp)

The stop is diagonally across from the Terminal 1 doors. The sign is explicit: a green arrow points to "Quai en face / Opposite Platform", meaning the platform on the other side of the road; below it the tram symbol carries a "2" and a "B", then "Vers / To: Terminal 2" and the words "Liaison gratuite / Free connection".

![The "Aéroport Terminal 1" tram stop: a slatted canopy, a display showing the next Terminal 2 service in two minutes, and a shelter below](./images/16.webp)

The platform has a canopy and benches, and the display tells you how many minutes until the next service. When the tram arrives, read the display on the front: services showing "AÉROPORT" are the line 2 trams heading for the airport, with Terminal 2 two stops away. If you board one going into the city by mistake, get off at the next stop and come back — it costs time, not money.

![A red and white tram at the platform, its front display reading "AÉROPORT"](./images/17.webp)

At Terminal 2, get off and follow the "Départs / Departures" signs into the terminal, using the screens to find your check-in island and security. Going home, reverse the sequence: tram from Terminal 2 to Terminal 1, across the drop-off lane, along the pavement to Saint-Augustin, and the train back into Nice.

The tram runs in both directions and so frequently that the timetable hardly matters: by the time one leaves, the next is usually already showing. Between the two airport stops there is no ticket to buy and none to check — you simply get on. If your party is split between terminals, meeting at the Terminal 1 tram stop is far easier than finding each other inside the buildings.

The times, fares and connections here come from a traveller's account in October 2026; train times, fares and tram platforms are subject to the day's information on site and on the operator's website. Two last points: the ticket names a specific train, but according to the original note other services are sometimes accepted, so check with staff on the platform if unsure; and that 300–400 metre walk really is exposed, so do not treat it as a gentle stroll.
