Madrid's airport lies about 13 km from the city centre and handles around 68.2 million passengers a year (2025), making it one of Europe's busiest hubs. Its structure is **two terminal clusters about 2 km apart**: T1, T2 and T3 form one group, while **T4 and its satellite T4S form a second** — and a cross-cluster connection is the most common mistake travellers make here.

## The two terminal clusters

| Code | Name | Main carriers | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Star Alliance and SkyTeam international carriers | Mostly non-Schengen international flights |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | Air Europa and others | Mostly Schengen flights |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | Low-cost carriers | The smallest |
| T4 | Terminal 4 | Iberia and oneworld | Check-in, arrivals and Schengen flights |
| T4S | T4 Satellite | As above | Used for **long-haul and non-Schengen flights** |

**How to tell where you are:**

- **Iberia and oneworld are in the T4 cluster** — long-haul flights mostly leave from T4S, reached from T4 by an underground automated people mover in about 3–5 minutes.
- **T1, T2 and T3 are linked on the ground level**, so moving between the three can be done on foot.
- **The two clusters are not connected**, so a transfer means a free shuttle bus (24 hours, about 15–20 minutes) and a second security check.

> Our diagram draws the T4 cluster as a block separate from T1–T3, with T4S marked out on its own, precisely to show that cross-cluster distance.

## Transfers and connections

1. **Check first whether both flights use the same cluster** — staying within the T4 cluster (T4S included) or within T1–T3 makes for the quickest connection.
2. T4 and T4S are linked by the people mover; waiting plus the ride is about 10 minutes, so allow 90 minutes for a long-haul to long-haul connection.
3. **For a cross-cluster connection allow more than 2 hours**, because you will travel landside by shuttle and clear security again.

## Routes and flights

- **Routes** — around 90 airlines flying to about 200 destinations, and Europe's primary gateway to Latin America; long-haul points include Buenos Aires, São Paulo, Bogotá, Lima, New York, Los Angeles and Tel Aviv.
- **Flights** — 68.18 million passengers in 2025 (+3.0%, a record), with about 431,000 aircraft movements.
- **Aircraft** — main base is **Iberia and oneworld (T4/T4S)**, followed by Air Europa, Ryanair and Vueling; Iberia flies the A320 family plus A330/A350 widebodies.

## Getting into Madrid

- **Metro Line 8** — has its own stations at T1–T3 and at T4 and runs straight into the city's metro network; the cheapest option.
- **Cercanías line C-1** — **calls only at T4**, running direct to Atocha and Chamartín; the shortcut if you are changing to a mainline train.
- **Airport Express bus, route 203** — runs 24 hours direct to the city centre, and the reliable choice for a late-night arrival.
- **Taxi** — about 13 km, with a flat rate towards the city centre, so traffic does not change the price.

## Practical tips

- Madrid is Europe's main gateway to Latin America, and **almost all long-haul flights to Latin America leave from T4S**. Checking in at T4 and boarding at T4S is the norm, so arrive with the people-mover transfer time built in.
- You can only board the Cercanías C-1 at T4; if you are staying on the T1–T3 side, take the shuttle or the metro across to T4 first.
- Airlines move terminals from time to time, so before you set off, go by your boarding pass and the airport's official information.
