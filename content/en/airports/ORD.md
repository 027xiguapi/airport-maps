O'Hare sits about 27 km from the city centre and handles around 84.8 million passengers a year (2025). It is a **hub under construction**: **the terminals are numbered 1, 2, 3 and 5 — there is no Terminal 4** — and that single fact is the most common source of confusion here. At the same time the airport is carrying out its largest rebuild in decades.

## The four terminals

| Code | Name | Main users |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | United domestic and international flights |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | United plus a mix of other airlines |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | American Airlines and oneworld members |
| T5 | Terminal 5 | International flights (except some United international routes) |

**Why is there no 4?** The number was skipped historically, and the surviving "4" is only a stop on the airport's internal transit train, not a terminal. A boarding pass that says T5 is entirely normal — you have not missed T4.

- **ATS train** — free, linking every terminal, the parking garages and the car hire centre; this is the main way to change building.
- United is in T1/T2, American in T3 and most international flights in T5 — moving between the three means taking the ATS or a walking connector.

> Our diagram draws only the four buildings 1, 2, 3 and 5 and leaves the slot for 4 empty precisely to make that skipped number clear.

## Transfers and connections

1. Cross-terminal connections are the norm at O'Hare: United to American, or domestic to international, both need 2–3 hours.
2. **United to United** is completed within T1/T2 and **American to American** within T3 — the smoothest combinations.
3. T5 is reached from the other buildings by ATS, with a wait plus ride of about 15–20 minutes — build that into your buffer.

## Routes and flights

- **Routes** — around 50 airlines flying to about 250 destinations; long-haul points include London, Frankfurt, Paris, Madrid, Rome, Beijing, Shanghai, Tokyo, Seoul, Delhi, Tel Aviv, Toronto and Mexico City.
- **Flights** — 84.81 million passengers in 2025 (+6% year on year, sixth worldwide), with more than 857,000 aircraft movements — reclaiming the title of the world's busiest airfield.
- **Aircraft** — main bases are **United (T1/T2)** and **American Airlines (T3)**, followed by oneworld and Star Alliance carriers; the fleet is 737 and A320 family narrowbodies, with 787, 777 and A350 on long-haul.

## Getting into Chicago

- **CTA Blue Line** — **runs 24 hours**, about 45 minutes direct to the city centre, a reliable choice for a late arrival, and the fare is very low.
- **Taxi / ride-hailing** — about 27 km, 30 minutes without traffic, and markedly longer at peak times or in bad weather.
- **Metra commuter rail** — services link to the city centre, useful if you are staying in the western suburbs.
- **Airport buses** — cover the city centre hotel district.

## Practical tips

- The airport is in the middle of a large-scale rebuild (a new satellite concourse and the Global Terminal, built in phases), so **routes inside the buildings may be adjusted at short notice** — check the airport's or your airline's latest guidance before you travel.
- Chicago winters bring frequent snow and wind, and delays and de-icing waits are common, so allow generous margins for a winter connection.
- For a late-night arrival, take the Blue Line first — it is one of the few airport rail lines that runs through the night.
