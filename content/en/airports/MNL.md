Ninoy Aquino International Airport sits right beside Manila, about 7 km from the city centre, and handles around 52 million passengers a year (2025). It has **four terminals with no walkways and no rail link between them**, only road shuttles — which makes it one of the airports in Southeast Asia where you most need to leave generous time.

## The four terminals

| Code | Name | Main flights | Main carriers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Mostly international | Several foreign carriers; AirAsia Philippines international flights also use it |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | Domestic + international | Philippine Airlines only, split into domestic and international zones |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | International + domestic | The largest and newest; Cebu Pacific, JAL, ANA and others |
| T4 | Terminal 4 | Domestic | Small low-cost carriers and turboprop flights |

**How to tell where you are:**

- **For Philippine Airlines, look to T2**; for Cebu Pacific and most international carriers, T3; AirAsia Philippines flies its **international routes from T1 and its domestic routes from T2** (changed from March 2026).
- T1 / T2 / T3 are linked by a **free inter-terminal shuttle** (running 24 hours, **boarding pass required**), which uses public roads and is exposed to traffic.
- **T4 is the furthest from the others**, and the drive can take 30–40 minutes.

> Our diagram draws the four buildings as separate structures precisely to make the point that every terminal change here is a road journey.

## Transfers and connections

1. Manila has **no airside inter-terminal transit** — changing buildings means leaving the terminal, collecting your bags and checking in and clearing security again.
2. Official and airline advice: **2 hours for international-to-international in the same building, 3–4 hours for international-to-international across buildings, and 3.5–4 hours for international-to-domestic**.
3. The early morning (05:00–09:00) and the evening (16:00–20:00) are the congestion peaks, so always budget a cross-terminal connection against peak conditions.

## Routes and flights

- **Routes** — around 30-plus airlines flying to about 60 destinations; long-haul points include Tokyo, Osaka, Seoul, Beijing, Shanghai, Hong Kong, Singapore, Dubai, Sydney, Los Angeles and San Francisco.
- **Flights** — about 52.02 million passengers in 2025, an all-time record for the airport.
- **Aircraft** — main bases are **Philippine Airlines (T2)** and **Cebu Pacific (T3)**, followed by AirAsia Philippines and PAL Express; the fleet is the A320/A321 family, with A330 and A350 on long-haul.

## Getting into Manila

- **Taxi / rideshare (Grab)** — about 7 km, but **Manila's congestion can stretch that to an hour**, so allow for it.
- **Airport buses** — linking the main districts of the city with the terminals.
- **Light rail / metro** — you must first take road transport out of the airport area to reach it, which is not convenient for travellers with heavy luggage.

## Practical tips

- Manila is one of the Asian hubs that demands the most patience when connecting: **budget 4 hours rather than expecting 2 to cover a cross-terminal transfer**.
- In the holiday seasons (Holy Week, All Souls' Day, Christmas and New Year) the crowds stack up, and **queues for the inter-terminal shuttle get noticeably longer**.
- The city is only a street away from the airport but the congestion is severe, so when you have a flight to catch, plan the ground journey for the worst case.
