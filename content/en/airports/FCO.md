Fiumicino lies about 30 km from Rome's city centre and handles around 51.3 million passengers a year (2025), Italy's largest airport. Its terminal layout is simple: **T1 takes Schengen and domestic traffic, T3 takes long-haul international** — but there is one thing you must remember: **the airport railway station is at T3**, so passengers leaving from T1 have to walk to T3 before they can board the express.

## The two terminals

| Code | Name | Main flights | Main carriers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Domestic and intra-Schengen | ITA Airways and its partners |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | Long-haul international and non-Schengen | ITA, SkyTeam, Star Alliance and oneworld carriers |

**How to tell where you are:**

- **ITA domestic and Schengen flights use T1**; intercontinental and non-Schengen flights use T3.
- T1 and T3 are **joined airside by a walkway (the SkyBridge)**, so once through security you can walk between the two buildings.
- The airport railway station sits on the T3 side; from T1 you reach it on foot via the SkyBridge, or landside.

> Our diagram draws T1 and T3 as a pair of buildings joined by a walkway precisely to mark that airside route and where the station sits.

## Transfers and connections

1. First check which building each flight uses: both in T3 or both in T1 is the quickest.
2. **Between T1 and T3 you can use the airside walkway**, normally a walk of 10–15 minutes, with no need to clear security again.
3. Connecting between a Schengen and a non-Schengen flight means passport control, so leave a margin on top of that.

## Routes and flights

| Aspect | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Routes | In 2025 the airport connected Rome with about 80 countries and more than 240 destinations; 51.3 million passengers (+4%), the first time it has passed 50 million, and Italy's busiest |
| Flights | 51.3 million passengers in 2025; traditional carriers carried 37% and low-cost carriers 63% of the total |
| Aircraft | **ITA Airways** is the base carrier, flying A320-family aircraft and A330 widebodies on domestic, European and intercontinental routes; Ryanair and other low-cost carriers also operate extensively from T1 |

## Getting into Rome

- **Leonardo Express** — **the station is at T3**, 32 minutes direct to Roma Termini, every 15 minutes, at a fixed fare.
- **FL1 regional train** — calls at several city stations; a lower fare but many stops, which suits areas such as Trastevere.
- **Airport bus** — direct to Termini and the main choice at night, once the trains stop.
- **Taxi** — about 30 km, with a flat rate towards the city centre, so confirm the official price before you get in.

## Practical tips

- The Leonardo Express **stops running after about 23:30 and resumes around 05:00**, so take the airport bus or a taxi if you arrive late at night.
- Rome has **two airports**: Fiumicino (FCO, the main international one) and Ciampino (CIA, mostly low-cost carriers), so check the three-letter code when you book.
- If you are leaving from T1 and want the express into the city, **you must first walk to the station at T3** — build that walk into your timing.
