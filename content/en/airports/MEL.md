Melbourne Airport lies about 23 km from the city centre and handles around 36 million passengers a year (2024–25 financial year), making it Australia's second-busiest airport. Its terminal layout has a direct effect on how you travel: **T1, T2 and T3 stand in a row, with T4 off on its own to the south** — and because Qantas and Jetstar each use two of them, "find the terminal by airline" does not work here; you have to **look at whether the flight is domestic or international**.

## The four terminals

| Code | Name | Flight type | Main carriers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Domestic | Qantas and QantasLink |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | International | All international carriers |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | Domestic | Virgin Australia and others |
| T4 | Terminal 4 | Domestic | Jetstar and other low-cost and regional carriers |

**How to tell where you are:** **international flights are always in T2**; domestic flights split by airline — Qantas in T1, Virgin in T3, Jetstar and others in T4.

- Note in particular that **both Qantas and Jetstar use two buildings at once** — the same airline's domestic and international legs are not in the same building, so once you have booked, confirm the terminal for each leg.
- T1–T3 are directly connected on foot; **T4 is some distance from T1–T3**, linked by a walkway or a shuttle.

> Our diagram draws T1–T3 as one continuous row and T4 as a separate block precisely to show that "three together, one apart" arrangement.

## Transfers and connections

1. Locate your terminal by domestic or international first, and only then by airline.
2. An international-to-domestic connection means clearing immigration, collecting your bags and checking in again, and may involve a move between T2 and T1/T3/T4 — allow 3 hours.
3. When connecting between T4 and T1–T3, count the walkway or shuttle time into your buffer as well.

## Routes and flights

- **Routes** — around 30 airlines flying to about 70 non-stop destinations; long-haul points include Singapore, Bangkok, Hong Kong, Tokyo, Shanghai, Los Angeles, San Francisco, Dubai and Johannesburg.
- **Flights** — about 36 million passengers in the 2024–25 financial year, of which about 11.95 million were international; December 2025 set a single-month record of 3.42 million passengers.
- **Aircraft** — main bases are **Qantas (T1 domestic / T2 international), Virgin Australia (T3) and Jetstar (T4)**; domestic workhorses are the 737 and A320 families, with A330 and 787 on international long-haul.

## Getting into Melbourne

- **SkyBus** — about 30 minutes direct to Southern Cross Station in the city centre, running frequently and 24 hours a day; the choice most travellers make.
- **Taxi / rideshare** — about 23 km, and 30 minutes when traffic is clear.
- **Car hire** — counters in every terminal, popular with travellers driving out towards the Great Ocean Road.
- **Hotel shuttles** — some city hotels run a pick-up service.

## Practical tips

- SkyBus is the least fuss into town: Southern Cross connects to trams, trains and intercity coaches, and covers most of the city's accommodation areas.
- Melbourne's terminal numbers are historical, so do not puzzle over why Terminal 2 sits in the middle — finding your building by flight type is quicker.
- Domestic security and boarding in Australia are relatively simple, but low-cost baggage rules are strict, so check the baggage policy of the T4 carriers before you travel.
