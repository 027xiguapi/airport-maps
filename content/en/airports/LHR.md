Heathrow is one of Europe's busiest hubs, handling around 84 million passengers a year about 24 km from central London. It now operates **four terminals — 2, 3, 4 and 5** (Terminal 1 has closed), and **each terminal is closely tied to an airline alliance**, so which airline you fly largely decides which building you use.

## The four terminals in use

| Code | Name | Gates | Main users |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T2 | Terminal 2 (The Queen's Terminal) | A1–A20 | Star Alliance carriers |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | B1–B16 | oneworld carriers |
| T4 | Terminal 4 | C1–C14 | SkyTeam and some others |
| T5 | Terminal 5 | Concourses D / E / F | British Airways |

**Two practical consequences follow:**

- **A connection within the same alliance usually stays in one building**, which makes for the smoothest transfer.
- **A cross-alliance connection will most likely change terminals**, so allow for it.

> Note the gate lettering: T2 uses A, T3 uses B, T4 uses C, and T5 uses the D, E and F concourses. The letter itself is a clue to the terminal.

## Changing terminals and connecting

Heathrow's terminals are linked by a **free airside/landside transfer**, but a terminal change plus a second security check is a real cost in time. Our advice:

1. Once ticketed, confirm the terminal for *both* flights — **do not rely on the airline name alone**.
2. For a cross-terminal connection, build the transfer and security time into your buffer; a same-terminal connection is far more forgiving.
3. Inside T5 the D, E and F concourses are some distance apart — if your gate is in F, set off early.

## Getting into central London

- **Heathrow Express** — about 15 minutes direct to Paddington, and the fastest option.
- **Piccadilly line** — direct to many central districts at a fraction of the price, but stops all the way in.
- **Coach** — National Express long-distance services, useful if you are heading straight to another city.
- **Taxi** — the black cab, available on demand, and the most expensive.

## Practical tips

- It is 24 km into the centre. Choosing between "fast" (Heathrow Express) and "cheap" (Piccadilly line) mostly comes down to luggage and arrival time.
- Paddington connects to the Underground and mainline rail, so for destinations beyond west London the Express is usually quicker than staying on the Tube.
- If your itinerary allows, pick flights that arrive and depart from the **same terminal** and you remove the terminal change entirely.
