Munich Airport lies about 28 km from the city centre and handles around 41 million passengers a year, making it Germany's second-busiest hub. Its logic is clean: **Lufthansa and Star Alliance have T2 to themselves, with every other carrier in T1** — and T2 also has a **satellite building** reachable only from inside the terminal, so a gate beginning with K or L means an extra hop on an underground people mover.

## Two terminals and a satellite

| Code | Name | Gates | Main carriers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Modules A–E | Every carrier outside the Lufthansa group |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | G (Schengen) / H (non-Schengen) | Lufthansa and Star Alliance members |
| 2S | Terminal 2 Satellite | K (Schengen) / L (non-Schengen) | Lufthansa group remote gates |

**How to tell where you are:**

- The Lufthansa group is in T2; every other carrier is in T1.
- A gate **K or L** means the satellite — it **can only be reached after security**, by an underground automated people mover from T2 that takes under 2 minutes and runs every few minutes.
- The two terminals are **not connected airside**, so a cross-terminal connection has to go landside and through security again.

> Our diagram draws the satellite as a building separate from T2 precisely to mark that people-mover link, which is airside-only.

## Transfers and connections

1. A Lufthansa-group connection (between T2 and the satellite) is the smoothest, usually done in 45–60 minutes.
2. **A T1-to-T2 connection takes the longest** — landside, through security again — so allow more than 2 hours.
3. Flights between inside and outside the Schengen area involve passport control, so add another notch of buffer on top.

## Getting into Munich

- **S-Bahn S1 / S8** — the station sits in the airport centre between the two terminals; about 40–45 minutes direct to the main station, running frequently.
- **Taxi** — about 28 km, and around 35 minutes when traffic is clear.
- **Airport buses** — covering the city and the surrounding towns.
- **Car hire** — counters in both terminals, handy for driving in Bavaria.

## Practical tips

- Munich has been expanding steadily, and **T1's new concourse opened in 2026** for non-Schengen flights; the routes inside may shift with it, so follow the signs on the day.
- A single S-Bahn ticket covers transport throughout the city, so changing to the U-Bahn or a tram in town needs no second ticket.
- Lufthansa long-haul flights usually board at the satellite, so a "gate K/L" means leaving time for the people mover rather than estimating T2 walking distances.
