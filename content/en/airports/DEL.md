Delhi's airport lies about 16 km from the centre of New Delhi and handles around 78 million passengers a year (2025), making it the largest aviation hub in South Asia. Its division of labour is unlike most hubs: **of the three terminals only T3 takes international flights**, while T1 and T2 are domestic-only — which means an international to domestic connection almost always involves a change of building.

## The three terminals

| Code | Name | Flight types | Main users |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Domestic only | Mostly low-cost carriers; capacity much increased after expansion |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | Domestic only | Some domestic trunk routes |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | International + domestic | The only international terminal; Air India and others |

**How to work out where you are going:** only T3 has international flights — **international arrivals are always at T3**; domestic flights are divided between T1, T2 and T3 by airline, and T3 has a domestic concourse of its own.

- T3's international concourses are A, B and C, and its domestic concourse is D — **an international to domestic connection inside the same building needs no exit**, and is the smoothest kind of transfer in Delhi.
- T1, T2 and T3 are some distance apart, and can only be linked landside by shuttle bus or rail.

> Our diagram lays the three buildings out separately precisely to make the point: in Delhi, it is the domestic leg's terminal that needs checking in advance, more than the international leg's.

## Transfers and connections

1. Start with the terminal of your domestic flight: if it is in the same building as T3 that is quickest; if it is at T1 or T2, allow transfer time.
2. An airside transfer bus now runs between the buildings (T1↔T3), so you do not have to clear immigration and go back in; transfer efficiency has improved noticeably in recent years.
3. International to international is almost always completed inside T3, where two hours is a comfortable buffer.

## Routes and flights

| Aspect | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Routes | The densest domestic network in India, serving well over a hundred domestic cities and the main long-haul international points (London, Dubai, Singapore, Bangkok and others); 78.15 million passengers in 2025, ranked 11th worldwide |
| Flights | Around 481,856 aircraft movements in 2025 (ACI), roughly 1,300 a day |
| Aircraft | **IndiGo** and **Air India** are the main bases; IndiGo flies A320-family aircraft on domestic trunks, while Air India uses widebodies (B777 / B787 / A350) on long-haul international |

## Getting into Delhi

- **Airport Express (Orange line)** — about 20 minutes direct to New Delhi station, with clean carriages and frequent services; the option we recommend most.
- **Other metro lines** — some run direct to T1; convenient for changes, but slower.
- **Taxi / ride-hailing** — prepaid counters and designated ride-hailing pickup areas, with transparent pricing; about 16 km.
- **Airport buses** — routes covering the city, and the lowest fare.

## Practical tips

- Delhi's air pollution is especially severe in winter and flight delays are common, so do not cut your connection too fine.
- Every terminal has prepaid taxi counters — **do not follow touts to a car at the arrivals exit**.
- For international flights, arrive three hours ahead: Delhi's airport has a number of security and document checks.
