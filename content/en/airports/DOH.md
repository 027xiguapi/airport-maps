Hamad International lies about 15 km from the centre of Doha and handles around 50 million passengers a year as Qatar Airways' global connecting hub. It has **a single terminal**, with five piers reaching out to either side — and as in Dubai or Istanbul, the logic of a connection here is that you walk within the building and never change building.

## One terminal, five piers

| Code | Name | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A / B / C | Piers | Boarding areas on either side of the main hall |
| D / E | Piers | Boarding areas added by the expansion, with the newest facilities |

**How to work out where you are going:** the gate letter is the pier. A–C are on the older side, while D and E belong to the expansion; a gate in those two zones means a longer walk.

- At the centre of the expansion is an indoor tropical garden, the Orchard — a signature sight, and a landmark to steer by during a long connection.
- Travelators link the building, and reaching the furthest pier takes 15–25 minutes.

> Our diagram draws it as a single hall with two long piers reaching out precisely to serve as a reminder: when your gate is in the D or E zone, count on another stretch of indoor walking.

## Transfers and connections

Doha is one of the world's most efficient transfer points, and a Qatar Airways through connection is close to seamless:

1. Every transfer is completed inside the same building, with no need to clear immigration or pass security again.
2. On a through ticket, 60–90 minutes is usually enough to connect, but **when your gates are in the D or E zone, stretch that to two hours**.
3. The transfer corridors and signage inside are clear — just follow the Transfer signs.

## Getting into Doha

- **Red Line metro** — there is an airport station, running direct to the Msheireb interchange in the city centre; fares are extremely low, making it the cheapest way.
- **Taxi** — a queued rank at the exit; about 15 km and 20 minutes or so, and not expensive.
- **Ride-hailing** — board at the designated point, and more convenient for a late-night arrival.

## Practical tips

- The Red Line runs until around midnight, so keep a taxi as your fallback if you arrive late at night.
- Doha is fiercely hot in summer; around midday, favour the metro and indoor routes and keep your time outdoors to a minimum.
- With time to spare on a connection, the rest areas, pool and garden inside the terminal are all worth exploring — there is no need to take a transit visa just to "go out and have a look".
