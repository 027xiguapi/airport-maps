Lisbon Airport lies only about 7 km from the city centre and handles around 36.1 million passengers a year (2025). It is one of Europe's rare "city airports", and structurally it has **one easy trap**: **Terminal 2 handles departures only, not arrivals** — if you fly out of T2 on a low-cost carrier but take the metro to T1, you still have a shuttle ride round to the other side ahead of you.

## The two terminals

| Code | Name | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | The main building, and **all arrivals** are here; departures for TAP Portugal and most airlines also leave from T1 |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | **Departures only**, used by low-cost carriers: Ryanair, Wizz, Vueling, Transavia and others |

**How to work out where you are:**

- **Arrivals are always at T1** — whichever airline you fly, you land, clear immigration and collect your baggage at T1.
- **Departures depend on the airline** — low-cost carriers are mostly at T2; T2 is about 1 km from T1, with **no footpath and no metro**, so the free shuttle bus is the only way across (roughly every 10–15 minutes, a few minutes' ride).

> Our diagram draws T2 as a separate small building from the main one precisely to mark this "outbound only" shuttle link.

## Transfers and connections

1. Lisbon is dominated by point-to-point traffic, and **a transfer across T1 and T2 means a shuttle and then security again**; allow more than 2.5 hours.
2. A transfer within T1 is straightforward, and connections within the TAP group save the most time.
3. Departing from T2, arrive earlier than usual — shuttle buses are limited and the wait is unpredictable.

## Routes and flights

- **Routes** — around 30 airlines flying to about 140 destinations, and southern Europe's most dense gateway to Brazil, Africa and North America; long-haul points include New York, Boston, São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro and Casablanca.
- **Flights** — about 36.1 million passengers in 2025, a record high (+3.4% year on year), and the busiest single-runway airport in Europe.
- **Aircraft** — main base is **TAP Air Portugal (T1)**, followed by Ryanair, Vueling and Wizz (T2 departures); TAP's workhorse fleets are the A320neo and A330neo families.

## Getting into Lisbon

- **Red metro line** — the station is inside the **T1 arrivals hall**, about 20 minutes into the city at a very low fare; passengers departing from T2 must go to T1 first and then take the shuttle.
- **Airport buses** — cover the main districts of the city centre and the railway station, with plenty of luggage space.
- **Taxi / rideshare** — only about 7 km, and 15 minutes or so when the traffic is clear.
- **Suburban rail** — reachable with a change in some directions, and useful for Sintra and other nearby towns.

## Practical tips

- One sentence is enough to remember: **arrivals at T1, low-cost departures at T2**. If you take the metro to T1 and need to move to T2, allow a 30-minute buffer.
- Lisbon Airport is planning relocation and expansion, so the long-term facilities may change; check official information before you travel.
- The city centre rises and falls sharply and the old streets are narrow, so with a lot of luggage a taxi is often less effort than the metro.
