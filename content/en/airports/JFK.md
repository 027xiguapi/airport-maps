JFK handles around 63 million passengers a year (2025) and sits about 24 km from Manhattan. Unlike most hubs it has **no single main terminal** — instead several separate terminals are spread around a ring road, which means *which terminal you land at* largely decides how long the trip into the city takes.

## The five terminals

| Code | Name | Gates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | 1–11 |
| T4 | Terminal 4 | Concourses A / B |
| T5 | Terminal 5 | 1–29 |
| T7 | Terminal 7 | 1–12 |
| T8 | Terminal 8 | Concourses A / B / C |

**Why do the numbers skip?** Because terminals that were demolished or rebuilt left gaps — **Terminals 2, 3 and 6 are no longer in use**. Seeing numbers jump is normal here; you have not missed one.

## Moving between terminals

**AirTrain JFK** is the spine that links the terminals to each other and to onward transport:

- It loops between all terminals and is free.
- It also connects to the **subway E / J / Z lines** and the **Long Island Rail Road (LIRR)** — the key link into New York.

So the standard route into the city is: **terminal → AirTrain → subway or LIRR**. For a cross-terminal connection you also rely on the AirTrain, so factor in the wait and the loop.

## Routes and flights

| Aspect | Details |

| Routes | The United States' leading international gateway; around 63 million passengers in 2025, with an especially dense transatlantic network to every major city worldwide |
| Flights | 1.6 million tonnes of air cargo in 2025; international passenger traffic remains the largest in the US |
| Aircraft | **Delta Air Lines** (T4) and **JetBlue** (T5) are the base carriers; **American Airlines** and British Airways and other oneworld carriers use T8, all flying widebodies such as the 767 / 777 / 787 and A321 / A330 / A350 on long-haul |

## Getting into Manhattan

- **AirTrain + subway** — the cheapest option. The AirTrain connects to the E / J / Z lines.
- **AirTrain + LIRR** — faster to Penn Station, but a higher fare than the subway.
- **Taxi** — yellow cabs charge a **flat rate** into Manhattan, with tolls and tips on top.
- **Airport buses** — link to the main districts of the city.
- **Car** — via the Van Wyck Expressway.

## Practical tips

- It is 24 km to Manhattan, but the real journey time swings heavily with traffic — **take the rail options when time matters**, and treat road traffic as the biggest source of uncertainty.
- The yellow-cab fare to Manhattan is flat, so **do not accept a verbally quoted price from an unlicensed driver**.
- Before connecting, check both terminal numbers: staying within T4 or within T8 is easiest, while a change of terminal means allowing for the AirTrain both ways.
