Guarulhos lies about 25 km from central São Paulo and handles around 43 million passengers a year, the busiest airport in South America. Its layout is unusually friendly to travellers: **the first digit of the gate number is the terminal number** — see 301 and you know you are in T3, see 205 and you are in T2. The difficulty is not reading it, but the fact that **T1 is not connected to T2/T3**.

## The three terminals

| Code | Name | Gate ranges | Main flights |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | 101–109 | Mostly domestic flights |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | 201–229 / 250–268 | Mixed domestic and international, LATAM's main base |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | 301–332 | International flights |

**How to tell where you are:** the first digit of the gate number is the terminal number — 1xx in T1, 2xx in T2, 3xx in T3.

- **T2 and T3 are joined by a walking connection** — about 10–15 minutes, indoors at both ends.
- **T1 is a separate building** — close to the railway station, and reaching T2/T3 means a free shuttle bus (roughly every 10–15 minutes during the day).

> Our diagram draws T1 as a separate block and T2 and T3 as a linked pair precisely to mark that relationship — one building apart, two joined.

## Transfers and connections

1. Confirm the terminal from the gate number first: a connection inside T2 or inside T3 is the quickest.
2. T2 and T3 can be linked on foot via the walking connection; allowing more than 30 minutes is safer.
3. A connection involving T1 needs the shuttle wait added on, so allow more than 2.5 hours.

## Getting into São Paulo

- **Airport bus** — direct to the city centre and the main hotel districts, with plenty of luggage space; the least stressful option.
- **Commuter rail + metro** — the airport has a railway station, but you first take a shuttle to reach it and then change again for the city; the lowest fare, with the most changes.
- **Taxi / ride-hailing** — about 25 km, 40 minutes without traffic; São Paulo's peaks are severely congested and the trip often takes twice as long.
- **Ride-hailing** — pick-up points are fixed and prices transparent, which makes it easier for a late-night arrival.

## Practical tips

- São Paulo is South America's largest city, and **the unpredictability of the roads matters far more than the distance itself**; if you are catching a flight, allow time for peak traffic.
- The airport is carrying out a large expansion: a new international terminal and the rebuilding of T2 are going ahead in stages, so **terminal arrangements may change** — check your airline's latest guidance before you set off.
- Domestic flying in Brazil is extremely dense and domestic transfer traffic is heavy, so check-in and security queues can be long; arrive early.
