Dublin Airport lies about 10 km from the city centre and handles around 36.4 million passengers a year (2025) as Ireland's gateway hub. It has two terminals and a simple structure; what is genuinely unusual is **an arrangement few other countries offer: passengers flying to the United States can clear US immigration at Dublin Airport before they leave** — on landing in the US, you walk out as a domestic passenger.

## The two terminals

| Code | Name | Main users |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Ryanair and other low-cost carriers, plus some European airlines |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | Aer Lingus and long-haul flights |

**How to work out where you are going:**

- **Aer Lingus and transatlantic long-haul flights mostly use T2**; low-cost carriers and most short-haul European flights use T1.
- The two buildings are adjacent, a few minutes apart on foot outdoors, with clear signage.

**For US flights, note especially:**

- Flights to the United States use **US preclearance** on the T2 side, in effect moving the immigration process forward to Dublin.
- As a result, **security and clearance take longer on these flights; arrive one hour earlier than you would for an ordinary international flight**.
- The payoff is that on landing in the US you exit through the domestic arrivals process and skip the long immigration queue.

## Transfers and connections

1. The two buildings are adjacent and a connection can be made on foot; for a change of terminal, allow two hours or more.
2. For a connection involving a US flight, check whether US clearance has to be completed in Dublin and allow extra time for it.
3. Aer Lingus is based here, and through connections run smoothly.

## Routes and flights

| Aspect | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Routes | Aer Lingus' main base, with transatlantic non-stops to the US East Coast and a dense European network; Ryanair also has a large base here; 36.43 million passengers in 2025 (+5.1%), a record year |
| Flights | Roughly 700 commercial movements a day; Aer Lingus alone carried about 11 million passengers through Dublin in 2025 |
| Aircraft | **Aer Lingus** (A320-family and A330 widebodies) operates from T2; **Ryanair** (Boeing 737) operates European low-cost services from T1 |

## Getting into Dublin

- **Airport coaches (Aircoach and others)** — covering the city centre and the main hotel districts, frequent and running 24 hours.
- **City buses (routes 16, 41 and others)** — the lowest fare, and they take a travel card; good if you are travelling light.
- **Taxi / ride-hailing** — only about 10 km and 20 minutes or so, and the least hassle.
- **Car hire** — good for a tour around the island (remember that Ireland drives on the left).

## Practical tips

- **Allow significantly more time for a US flight**: US clearance happens in Dublin and you cannot board without it, as the process is completed before departure.
- Dublin has a further convenience that is easy to miss: **on the way from the United States, some flights complete Irish immigration on the US side** (Shannon and Dublin have reciprocal arrangements).
- There are two main airports on the island of Ireland: Dublin (DUB) and Belfast (BFS, in the United Kingdom); check the three-letter code on cross-border itineraries.
