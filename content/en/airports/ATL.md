Atlanta's airport lies about 16 km from the city centre and handles **around 106 million passengers a year (2025), long ranked first in the world**. Its layout is exceptionally orderly: a single central spine threads **seven parallel concourses** lettered from T to F, with a 24-hour **Plane Train** running underground to link them all — so at Atlanta you almost never end up in the wrong building, only too far from your gate.

## The seven concourses

| Code | Name | Main use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T | Main concourse | Where check-in and security are, plus some domestic gates |
| A / B / C / D | Domestic concourses | Mainly Delta domestic flights |
| E / F | International concourses | International flights, including long-haul from Concourse F |

**How to work out where you are going:**

- The **letter of your gate is the concourse** — check the letter before boarding the train, and do not get off at the wrong one.
- **The Plane Train is the core artery** — the underground people mover runs 24 hours with trains every few minutes, linking T with all of A–F; a full run end to end takes about 10 minutes.
- International flights are concentrated in E and F, and most domestic connections between concourses also rely on the Plane Train.

> Our diagram draws it as a single spine with seven parallel concourses precisely to show that the cost in time here depends on how many concourses you cross, not how many buildings you change.

## Transfers and connections

1. **Delta's home base** — Delta connections run very smoothly here, usually achievable in around an hour.
2. For a domestic-to-international connection (E/F), count the Plane Train ride in; allow 2 hours.
3. The airport's traffic is enormous but the circulation is simple and clear — just follow the Plane Train signs.

## Routes and flights

| Aspect | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Routes | Delta runs nearly 1,000 peak-day departures to **207 destinations worldwide** (61 international), covering the US domestic network plus Europe, Latin America and Asia |
| Flights | **807,625 total aircraft movements** in 2025, second in North America and among the world's highest |
| Aircraft | **Delta Air Lines** is overwhelmingly the home carrier, with a mainline fleet of around 960 aircraft; main types are the A321neo, A330, A350 and Boeing 737, 757 and 767 |

## Getting into Atlanta

- **MARTA rail (red / gold lines)** — the station is on one side of the main building, about 20 minutes direct to the city centre with very low fares; the recommended option.
- **Taxi / ride-hailing** — about 16 km, picked up at designated points; the freeway is noticeably congested at peak times.
- **Car hire** — the rental centre is reached by people mover and is one of the largest airport rental facilities in the United States.
- **Airport buses** — serving the city centre and the surrounding areas.

## Practical tips

- Atlanta is the world's busiest airport, so **err on the generous side with connection times**: for a connection crossing three or more concourses, allow 1.5 hours.
- MARTA's operating hours are limited, so have a taxi or ride-hailing plan for a late-night arrival.
- After an international arrival, a domestic connection means clearing immigration, collecting your bags and passing customs before security and bag drop again; allow more than 2.5 hours.
