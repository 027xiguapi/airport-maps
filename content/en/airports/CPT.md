Cape Town International lies about 20 km from the city centre and handles around 10 million passengers a year, making it South Africa's second-busiest airport. Its structure is compact: **a single central terminal**, with the domestic and international sides set apart from each other but joined by a central concourse — **five to ten minutes on foot between them**, which is its greatest convenience. But there is one important limit: **the airport has no international transit corridor that bypasses immigration**.

## One terminal, two zones

| Zone | Location | Gates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| International | North side of the terminal | A1–A5 |
| Domestic | South side of the terminal | C1–C5 |
| Central concourse | Links the two sides | All check-in is handled on the upper level |

**How to work out where you are going:**

- International flights are on the north side and domestic flights on the south, five to ten minutes apart on foot through the central concourse — **no shuttle bus needed**.
- Check-in desks are gathered on the upper level of the central terminal, arrivals on the ground level, and domestic and international arrivals share a single exit hall.

## Transfers and connections

1. **There is no transit corridor that bypasses immigration** — whether you are connecting domestic to international or international to international, you must **clear immigration, collect your luggage, and check in and clear security again**.
2. For a **self-transfer on separate tickets, allow 2.5 hours or more**; on a through ticket, follow your airline's guidance.
3. International to international also requires entering South Africa, which is not the case at many hubs, so check your visa requirements in advance.

## Getting into Cape Town

- **Taxi / ride-hailing** — about 20 km, 25–35 minutes, and the most common way in.
- **Airport bus (MyCiTi connection)** — links the city centre and the main hubs, and the fare is low, but it requires a change.
- **Car hire** — there is a rental centre at the airport; Cape Town is the starting point of a Garden Route drive and demand for cars is high.
- **No rail** — the airport has no train or metro running into the city.

## Practical tips

- The airport is carrying out a large expansion running 2026–2029 (including a new runway and extra terminal capacity), so **parts of it are under construction** and routes through the building may change.
- Cape Town is the starting point of Garden Route and wine-country trips, so **allow for the queue at car collection** on a self-drive itinerary (it gets long in high season).
- South Africa's domestic network is dense, and travellers connecting from a domestic flight to an international one should budget 2.5 hours or more for the "no transit corridor" process.
