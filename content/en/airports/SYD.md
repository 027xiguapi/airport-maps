Sydney Airport is about 8 km from the city centre and handles **around 43 million passengers a year**, which makes it one of the closest airports to the centre of any major national capital. Its layout is one large terminal and two small ones: **T1 takes all international flights on its own, while T2 and T3 sit side by side handling domestic flights** — the two precincts face each other across a runway and cannot be reached on foot.

## The three terminals

| Code | Name | Main users | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | International Terminal | International carriers | All international flights |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | Virgin Australia, Jetstar and others | Domestic flights |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | Qantas | Qantas domestic only |

**Working out where you are going:** international is always T1; for domestic, go by the airline — Qantas is in T3, most others in T2. T2 and T3 are adjacent, a minute's walk apart.

> Our diagram draws T1 and T2/T3 as two blocks divided by a runway, precisely to remind you that the international-to-domestic transfer is not "next door" but "a tunnel under the road plus a train or a shuttle".

## Transfers and connections

- International to domestic means **clearing immigration, collecting your bags and checking in and clearing security again**; the two precincts are linked by a free shuttle bus or the train, so allow 3 hours for the connection.
- Qantas to Qantas (T3, or the Qantas area of T1) is the smoothest connection; for a transfer within one precinct, plan on 90 minutes.

## Routes and flights

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Routes | A record **around 43 million passengers in 2025**, of which a record **17.2 million were international**; **around 90 destinations** domestic and international, with long-haul points such as Los Angeles, San Francisco, London, Singapore, Tokyo and Dubai. |
| Flights | A record year, with movements at roughly **800 a day** (about 290,000 a year); international traffic grew 6.6% year on year in 2025. |
| Aircraft | The main base is **Qantas** (oneworld; about 125 aircraft — A330, 787 and a handful of A380 widebodies, plus 737 narrowbodies), followed by **Virgin Australia** (T2) and **Jetstar** (Qantas Group; A320 and 787, T2). |

## Getting to Sydney

- **Train** — there is a station at the international terminal and one at the domestic precinct; about 13 minutes direct to Central, with frequent services, and the first choice into the city.
- **Taxi** — only about 8 km, around 20 minutes, and better value than the train if there are several of you.
- **Airport buses** — cover the city and its surroundings.

## Practical tips

- Train fares include an airport access fee; in central Sydney you can tap in and out with a contactless credit card, so there is no need to buy a card.
- After clearing arrivals at T1, confirm whether your next flight is from T2 or T3 — they are adjacent, but the gate levels do not connect, so you go down and back in.
- The run into the city can stretch to 40 minutes in the evening peak, so build the traffic into your plans if you are catching a show or a long-distance coach.
