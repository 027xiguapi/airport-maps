Seattle-Tacoma sits about 23 km from the city centre, handles around 50 million passengers a year and is the gateway to the American north-west. Its layout is **four concourses plus two satellite buildings**: concourses A, B, C and D ring the main terminal, while the **North Satellite (N) and South Satellite (S)** stand apart and are linked by an underground train — if your gate letter carries an N or an S, you have a ride ahead of you.

## Concourses and satellites

| Code | Name | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A / B / C / D | Main terminal concourses | Ring the central terminal and are reached on foot |
| N | North Satellite | A separate building, reached by underground train |
| S | South Satellite | A separate building, reached by underground train |

**How to tell where you are going:**

- The gate letter is the location — **A–D are walkable; N / S need the underground train** (about 10 minutes including the wait).
- Alaska Airlines and Delta are the main carriers here, with an exceptionally well-developed domestic network.

> Our diagram draws the two satellite buildings as structures set apart from the main terminal precisely to mark out that underground rail link.

## Transfers and connections

1. Connections are completed within the same building complex; if yours crosses to a satellite (N/S), add the train time to your buffer.
2. Trains between the main terminal and the satellites are frequent, and a transfer usually takes 10–15 minutes.
3. Seattle is a starting point for Alaska and for the North Pacific routes to Asia, and through-connection signage is clear.

## Getting into Seattle

- **Link light rail** — the station connects the terminal and the car park, about 40 minutes direct to the city centre; the fare is low and traffic cannot affect it, making it the option we would recommend first.
- **Airport buses / express coaches** — cover the city and the surrounding towns.
- **Taxi / ride-hailing** — about 23 km, from designated pickup points; Interstate 5 is heavily congested at peak times.
- **Car hire** — a shuttle runs to the car hire centre, useful for driving to Mount Rainier and the Pacific coast.

## Practical tips

- The light rail runs from the airport straight to the city centre and on towards the University of Washington, and is **more reliable than the roads late at night and at peak times** — a standard choice for locals.
- Seattle is the gathering point for Alaska cruises and for flights into the interior, and **passenger numbers concentrate in cruise season (May–September)**, so arrive 3 hours ahead.
- Parts of the main terminal are under long-term renovation, so routes and shops may be adjusted — watch for the signs on site.
