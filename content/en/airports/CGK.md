Soekarno-Hatta sits north-west of Jakarta, about 20 km from the city centre, and handles around 55 million passengers a year (2025) as Indonesia's gateway hub. Its three terminals stand apart from each other with **no airside connection between them**, tied together by a free skytrain known locally as the **Kalayang** — that is the first thing to understand about this airport.

## The three terminals

| Code | Name | Main flights | Main users |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Domestic | Mostly low-cost: Lion Air, Citilink and others |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | Domestic + international | Full-service carriers; domestic at 2D, international at 2E/2F |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | Mainly international | The newest and largest; the main building for Garuda and international airlines |

**How to work out where you are going:**

- **Most international flights use T3**, with some at T2; domestic flights are spread across T1, T2 and T3 by airline.
- The three buildings are **not connected airside** — moving between them means leaving the building and taking the Kalayang skytrain or a shuttle bus.
- **The Kalayang is free**, runs roughly 06:00–24:00 every few minutes, and is replaced by shuttle buses at night.

> Our diagram draws the three buildings as a group joined by a single elevated track precisely to mark out that free skytrain.

## Transfers and connections

1. First confirm whether both flights use the same building — staying within one building saves the most time.
2. **For a connection between buildings, allow 60–90 minutes or more** — you have to leave the building, wait for the train and clear security again.
3. International to domestic is the common connection here, so count both security checks and the ride between buildings into your time.

## Routes and flights

| Aspect | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Routes | Around 54.95 million passengers in 2025, the busiest airport in South-East Asia for domestic traffic; it serves cities across the Indonesian archipelago plus international points in South-East and East Asia, the Middle East and Australia |
| Flights | **229,249 aircraft movements** in 2025 |
| Aircraft | **Garuda Indonesia** is the home-based full-service carrier, with around 99 serviceable aircraft in the group (end of 2025); main types are the Boeing 737, 777-300ER and Airbus A330; low-cost carriers Lion Air, Citilink and Indonesia AirAsia also operate heavily |

## Getting into Jakarta

- **Airport rail link (Railink)** — the station sits between T1 and T2 and is reached by the Kalayang; about 45–60 minutes direct to BNI City in the city centre, every 30 minutes, and **the best way to avoid the congestion on the ground**.
- **Airport buses** — reach several hubs in the city, and cost the least.
- **Taxi / ride-hailing** — board at the designated points; Jakarta's congestion is famous the world over, and **the morning and evening peaks can double your journey time**.

## Practical tips

- Jakarta is the archetype of the gridlocked city: **take rail whenever you can** — the airport rail link and the Kalayang are free or cheap and run to time.
- There is **no walking connection between the three terminals**, so do not estimate your time on the basis that it is only a few steps.
- Indonesia's domestic network is enormous, and travellers connecting from a domestic flight to an international one should pay particular attention to the terminal code (2D is domestic, 2E/2F is international).
