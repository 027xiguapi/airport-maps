Kansai International Airport is built on an artificial island in Osaka Bay, about 38 km from central Osaka, and handles around 34 million passengers a year (2025). Its structure is not complicated, but **the two buildings serve entirely different traffic**: **the T1 main building across the sea bridge carries every traditional airline**, while **T2 on the far side of that bridge is the low-cost terminal** — the terminal number on your boarding pass decides directly whether you have an extra shuttle bus ride ahead of you.

## The two terminals

| Code | Name | Main carriers | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | JAL, ANA and the international airlines | Domestic and international flights |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | Peach and other low-cost carriers | LCC only |

**How to work out where you are:** full-service airlines are all at T1; see Peach and other low-cost carriers and they are almost always at T2. T2 has no direct link to the bridge, so from the railway station side you reach it by **free shuttle bus**.

> Our diagram draws T1 as the signature extra-long slab and T2 as a separate small building precisely to mark the shuttle link between the two.

## Transfers and connections

- A T1-to-T2 transfer needs the shuttle bus; allow more than 90 minutes for the connection. A transfer within one building saves the most time.
- International-to-domestic and domestic-to-international transfers are both completed inside the T1 main building, with the process concentrated under one roof.

## Routes and flights

| Aspect | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Routes | The gateway to the Kansai region, operating 24 hours a day; about 33.55 million passengers in fiscal year 2025, of which 21.2 million were international foreign visitors, a record; the network covers East and Southeast Asia, North America and Hawaii |
| Flights | Around 33.55 million passengers in fiscal year 2025, with international and domestic traffic roughly balanced |
| Aircraft | **Peach Aviation** is one of the main bases, with the low-cost T2 terminal dedicated to it; JAL, ANA and ANA Wings also fly A320-family aircraft from T1 |

## Getting into Osaka

- **Nankai Rapi:t** — a limited express reaching Namba in about 38 minutes, with carriages and a timetable both designed around travellers.
- **JR Haruka limited express** — about 30 minutes to Tennoji and about 50 minutes to Shin-Osaka, with some services running through to Kyoto in about 80 minutes; useful if you are continuing elsewhere in western Japan.
- **Airport buses** — cover the main districts of Osaka and Kobe.
- **Taxi** — a considerable distance once across the bridge, so it only pays when shared.

## Practical tips

- Osaka also has **Itami Airport (ITM)**, which handles domestic routes only. When a search turns up "Osaka", always check the three-letter code: international and low-cost flights are mostly at Kansai (KIX), while ANA and other domestic flights are mostly at Itami.
- T2's facilities are pared back, with fewer places to eat and fewer check-in counters than T1, so arrive earlier still for a low-cost flight.
- On an artificial-island airport, strong winds can bring speed limits on the bridge, so if you are pressed for time choose the railway rather than the bus.
