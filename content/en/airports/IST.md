Istanbul Airport lies about 35 km from the city centre and handles around 84 million passengers a year (2025), making it one of the world's busiest airports by flight numbers. What is most unusual about it is this: **the whole airport is one building** — a terminal of roughly 1.4 million square metres forming a single continuous whole, domestic and international under the same roof, with five piers reaching out from the central hall.

## One terminal, five piers

| Code | Name | Main use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A / B / D / F | Piers (international) | Mostly international flights; the F area also holds three domestic gate zones |
| G | Pier (domestic) | Domestic flights only, with its own security and immigration |

**How to work out where you are:** the letter of your gate is the letter of your pier. International flights look to A / B / D / F, and domestic flights are almost all in the G area.

- **There is no people-mover inside** — the piers are linked entirely by walking and travelators, and the furthest gates are 20–30 minutes away.
- **A connection never changes building** — whether domestic to international or international to international, it is all completed under the same roof, and that is the core advantage of this airport as a hub.

> Our diagram draws it as one enormous main building with five radiating piers precisely to make the point: here you will only walk far, never end up in the wrong building.

## Transfers and connections

- All connections are completed inside the building, but **distance itself is the biggest variable** — build the walk between piers into your connecting margin.
- A domestic-to-international transfer requires passing through security and immigration once, so allow more than 2 hours; international-to-international is usually smoother.
- Wayfinding inside the building is clear, and although the hub is vast the route is a single one — simply follow your gate letter.

## Routes and flights

| Aspect | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Routes | Serving more than 330 destinations; 84 million passengers in 2025 (+5.5%), with about 48% of passengers transferring — among the busiest airports in Europe |
| Flights | Around 547,000 aircraft movements in 2025, nearly 1,500 a day |
| Aircraft | **Turkish Airlines** is the base carrier, with a fleet of around 500 aircraft (516 at the end of 2025) flying Boeing 737 / 777 and Airbus A320 / A330 / A350 across its global network |

## Getting into the city

- **Metro line M11** — runs straight into the city's metro network and is the best way to avoid the traffic; operating hours are limited, so for a late-night arrival make other plans.
- **HAVAIST airport coaches** — routes cover Taksim, Sultanahmet and other main districts, with frequent departures.
- **Taxi** — about 35 km and roughly 45 minutes when the traffic is clear; Istanbul's peak-hour congestion is severe and the journey can take twice as long.

## Practical tips

- Istanbul has a second airport, **Sabiha Gökçen Airport (SAW)**, on the Asian side, where most low-cost carriers fly. Always check the three-letter code when booking: if you are staying on the Asian side, SAW may be the nearer option.
- Passenger volumes are enormous and check-in and security queues are substantial, so arrive 3 hours ahead for an international flight.
- The building's shops and rest areas are on a huge scale, and a long connection does not require clearing immigration — when time is tight, rest where you are.
