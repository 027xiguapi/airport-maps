Kuala Lumpur International Airport lies about 50 km from the city centre and handles around 63.4 million passengers a year (2025). The key to it is that **"KUL" is not one building**: the KLIA main terminal used by traditional airlines, its satellite gate building, and the low-cost **klia2** are three facilities set apart from one another — the terminal name on your boarding pass decides directly which of them you are heading for.

## The main terminal complex and klia2

| Area | Name | Main carriers | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| KLIA Main | Main Terminal Building | Malaysia Airlines and full-service airlines | Check-in, security, immigration |
| Satellite | Satellite Building | As above | Some international gates, linked to the main building by a people-mover |
| klia2 | Low-cost terminal | AirAsia and others | A separate building, set apart from the main building |

**How to work out where you are:** full-service airlines are at KLIA; see AirAsia and other low-cost codes and they are almost always at klia2.

- **Aerotrain** — links the KLIA main building to the satellite building; free, running 24 hours, every few minutes. When your gate is in the satellite building, take it after security.
- klia2 and the main building are **not within walking distance**, so you reach klia2 by one stop on the KLIA Ekspres or by shuttle bus.

> Our diagram draws the KLIA main building, the satellite building and klia2 as three separate structures precisely to serve as a reminder: the "Kuala Lumpur" on your low-cost leg is in another building.

## Transfers and connections

1. **First check whether both flights use the same complex** — both at KLIA (main building plus satellite) is smoothest; a connection involving klia2 has to add the time to change buildings.
2. From the KLIA main building to the satellite you rely on the people-mover, about 10 minutes including the wait, so count it into your margin.
3. For a transfer across KLIA and klia2, allow more than 3 hours, especially when it involves clearing immigration, collecting baggage and checking in again.

## Routes and flights

- **Routes** — around 70 airlines in 2025, with 40 new routes launched; coverage spans Southeast Asia, China, South Asia, Oceania and the Middle East, and long-haul points include London, Sydney, Tokyo, Seoul and Dubai.
- **Flights** — 63.4 million passengers in 2025, up about 11% year on year; domestic traffic grew 17% and international 8.7%.
- **Aircraft** — main bases are **Malaysia Airlines (full service, main terminal)** and **AirAsia (low-cost, klia2)**; AirAsia flies mainly the A320 family, while Malaysia Airlines uses A330 and A350 widebodies on long-haul.

## Getting into Kuala Lumpur

- **KLIA Ekspres** — non-stop to KL Sentral in the city centre in about 28 minutes, and the fastest way into the city from the airport.
- **KLIA Transit** — the same line stopping at every station, with a lower fare, and you can change to other lines en route.
- **Airport buses** — cover KL Sentral and points across the city; the lowest fare, but exposed to road conditions.
- **Taxi** — buy your ticket before boarding the car; about 50 km and an hour or so.

## Practical tips

- klia2 is AirAsia's main base and its facilities are utilitarian, with food and rest areas more compact than the main building's — **arrive earlier still for a low-cost flight**.
- The KLIA Ekspres stops at both KLIA and klia2, so you can change buildings one stop on it, more punctual than the bus.
- Kuala Lumpur also has facilities such as Subang Airport (SZB), so check that the three-letter code and the airport really are KUL when booking.
