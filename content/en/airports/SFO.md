San Francisco International Airport sits about 21 km from the city centre and handles around 50 million passengers a year. Its four terminals are laid out in a U: **three domestic terminals (Harvey Milk T1, T2 and T3) plus one international terminal** — but the international terminal is itself split into two boarding areas, A and G, at opposite ends of the U, and that is the structure most easily overlooked at this Bay Area airport.

## The four terminals

| Code | Name | Boarding area | Main users |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Harvey Milk Terminal 1 | B | Delta, Alaska, Southwest, JetBlue and others |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | D | Air Canada, WestJet and some United domestic flights |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | E / F | United domestic only |
| T4 | International Terminal | A / G | All international flights |

**How to tell where you are going:** international gates always carry an **A or G prefix**, and the two sit at opposite ends of the terminal:

- **Area G** connects to T3 and **Area A** connects to Harvey Milk T1.
- **There is no direct passage between Areas A and G**; crossing on foot means passing through the terminals in between, about 20–25 minutes.

> Our diagram draws the International Terminal as one end of the U and marks the A and G boarding areas at its two ends precisely to show that distance — one building, two separate areas.

## Transfers and connections

1. First see whether the international gate is A or G, then confirm which building the domestic leg uses.
2. **Area G with T3 (United) and Area A with T1** are the smoothest pairings, completed inside the building without clearing security.
3. Moving from G to A is a long-distance connection: allow more than 90 minutes, or take the airside walkway instead.

## Getting into San Francisco

- **BART** — the station is inside the International Terminal (Level 4), about 30 minutes direct to the city centre, and the first choice for getting into town.
- **AirTrain** — free, running 24 hours and every few minutes, connecting the four terminals, the car parks and the BART station.
- **Taxi / ride-hailing** — from designated pickup points, about 21 km; US 101 is heavily congested at peak times.
- **Caltrain connection** — one BART stop to Millbrae and then a change, good for the Silicon Valley direction.

## Practical tips

- The Bay Area has a dense cluster of airports: Oakland (OAK) and San José (SJC) are both nearby, so check the three-letter code when booking or arranging a pickup.
- From the International Terminal to the domestic terminals you can stay indoors the whole way, using the walkways plus the AirTrain — no need to leave the building in the rain.
- BART runs until around midnight; for a late arrival, have a taxi or ride-hailing plan ready.
