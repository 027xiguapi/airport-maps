Cairo International lies about 15 km from the city centre and handles around 28 million passengers a year, making it a major gateway hub for Africa. It is made up of three terminals that **are not connected to each other but are linked by a free 24-hour inter-terminal people mover (APM)** — and that is the key to understanding Cairo's airport.

## The three terminals

| Code | Name | Main carriers | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Gulf and African carriers and others | An earlier building, mainly international flights |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | Several international airlines | Reopened after refurbishment, mixed use |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | EgyptAir and Star Alliance members | The largest and most modern, opened in 2009 |

**How to work out where you are going:** EgyptAir and its Star Alliance partners are mostly in T3; Gulf and African carriers mostly in T1; and T2, after its refurbishment, handles a mix. **Airline allocations change often, so go by your boarding pass and the flight displays.**

- **APM inter-terminal people mover** — free, running 24 hours between T1, T2 and T3, about 5 minutes direct with a service every few minutes, connecting on the landside.
- Walking between the three buildings is not realistic, even without luggage, so take the people mover to change terminals.

## Transfers and connections

1. **First confirm which building each flight uses** — EgyptAir connections are mostly made inside T3, which saves the most time.
2. A cross-terminal connection means going landside, taking the APM and going through security again; allow more than 2.5 hours.
3. Connections at Cairo are less efficient than at the Gulf hubs, so it is better to over-allow than to under-allow.

## Getting into Cairo

- **Taxi** — about 15 km; agree the price at an official desk before getting in, and do not accept a verbal offer from someone touting for custom.
- **Ride-hailing (Careem / Uber)** — transparent pricing and fixed pick-up points, and the least stressful option for arriving passengers.
- **Airport buses** — link to the main districts of the city centre at the lowest fare, though timings and road conditions are less predictable.
- **Metro** — the metro extension to the airport is still at the study and construction stage, and **there is currently no direct metro service to the airport**, so plan on travelling by road.

## Practical tips

- A fourth terminal is being planned to expand capacity, and the phased works may affect circulation inside some buildings; watch for signs on the ground.
- Book taxis and ride-hailing through the official desk or the app, and avoid getting into a car with anyone touting at the arrivals exit.
- Cairo's summers are hot with occasional dust storms; keep outdoor transfer time to a minimum around midday.
