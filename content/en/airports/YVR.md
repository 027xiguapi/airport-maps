Vancouver Airport sits about 12 km from the city centre and handles around 26 million passengers a year, making it Canada's second-busiest airport. Its main terminal is a clever piece of design: **domestic and international flights share a single building**, divided by stacked levels, so passengers can connect on foot; a separate **South Terminal** is dedicated to regional routes and seaplanes.

## The main terminal and the South Terminal

| Area | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Main Terminal · Domestic | Flights within Canada |
| Main Terminal · International | International flights, including the dedicated US-bound channel |
| South Terminal | Regional routes and seaplanes; separate from the main building |

**Working out where you are going:**

- The overwhelming majority of flights use the **Main Terminal**; the domestic and international zones are linked by indoor walkways, so **you never leave the building to connect**.
- **US-bound flights have a channel of their own**: you clear US immigration (pre-clearance) at Vancouver, and on landing in the US you exit as a domestic arrival.
- The South Terminal is separate from the main building, so passengers on regional flights or seaplanes should check their ground transport separately.

> Our diagram draws domestic and international as different zones of one building precisely to make the point: here you change zone within a building rather than trek between buildings.

## Transfers and connections

1. A domestic-to-international connection is made inside the same building — walking plus a security check usually connects within the hour.
2. **Allow pre-clearance time on US-bound flights** — 30–60 minutes earlier than for an ordinary international flight is the sensible margin.
3. Vancouver is the hub for Asia and western Canada, and Air Canada group itineraries connect smoothly.

## Getting into Vancouver

- **Canada Line** — about 25 minutes direct to the city centre, with frequent service, and the most recommended option.
- **Airport bus** — covers the downtown hotel district and towards the cruise terminal.
- **Taxi / ride-hailing** — about 12 km, 20–30 minutes into the city, with fares charged at a fixed rate by zone.
- **Seaplane** — departs from the South Terminal side direct to the downtown harbour; a distinctive option when time is tight.

## Practical tips

- **The biggest win for US-bound travellers is pre-clearance**: finish the immigration formalities in Vancouver and you do not queue again on landing in the US.
- Vancouver is the main home port for Alaska cruises, and **the airport gets busy in cruise season** — cruise passengers should set aside their port transfer time separately.
- For Whistler and other mountain destinations the airport bus runs direct services; in winter, allow extra for snowy road conditions.
