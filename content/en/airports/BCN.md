Barcelona's airport lies about 15 km from the city centre and handles around 57.5 million passengers a year (2025), making it Spain's second-largest airport. Its two terminals **sit about 4 km apart**: T1 is the main building and takes most airlines, while **T2 is made up of three modules, T2A, T2B and T2C, and is where most low-cost carriers go** — pick the wrong building and you face a long loop round by shuttle.

## The two terminals

| Code | Name | Main carriers | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Iberia, major international airlines | The main building, with the fullest facilities |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | Ryanair, easyJet and other low-cost carriers | Split into three modules, A / B / C |

**How to work out where you are going:**

- **Low-cost carriers mostly use T2** and full-service airlines mostly T1 — check the airline first, then the terminal number on your boarding pass.
- Between the two buildings **the free shuttle is the only option** (roughly every 10–15 minutes); there is no walkway.

> Our diagram draws T1 and T2 as two blocks facing each other across the runway precisely to mark that shuttle link.

## Transfers and connections

1. **The two terminals are not connected airside** — a cross-terminal connection means leaving the building, taking the shuttle and going through security again; allow more than 3 hours.
2. A connection inside T1 runs smoothly; 2 hours is a comfortable allowance.
3. Barcelona is a major gateway to Catalonia and to Mediterranean cruises, so cruise passengers should set aside time for the port leg separately.

## Routes and flights

| Aspect | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Routes | 57.48 million passengers in 2025, of whom 43.3 million were international; Barcelona–Palma (BCN–PMI) is Europe's busiest airport pair |
| Flights | Around **361,000 aircraft movements** in 2025 |
| Aircraft | **Vueling** is the largest base carrier (about 38% market share), with a group fleet of around 140 all-Airbus A320-family aircraft; about 60 are based at the Barcelona hub on more than 120 routes; Iberia, Ryanair and easyJet also operate heavily |

## Getting into Barcelona

- **Metro L9 Sud** — **has stations at both T1 and T2** and connects to the city's metro network; the cheapest option.
- **Suburban railway line R2** — **the station is at T2**, running direct to Plaça de Catalunya and Barcelona Sants; from T1 you must take the shuttle first.
- **Aerobús airport bus** — departs from T1 and T2 separately, direct to Plaça de Catalunya, with frequent services.
- **Taxi** — about 15 km, with a minimum fare for trips towards the city centre.

## Practical tips

- Remember the iron rule: **rail and bus stops are separate by terminal** — if you are departing from T2, use the T2 side, because crossing between terminals costs time and easily loses you your service.
- Barcelona has two further regional airports, **Girona (GRO) and Reus (REU)**, where some low-cost flights operate; always check the three-letter code when booking.
- In cruise season (April–October) the airport's traffic concentrates, so cruise passengers should set aside half a day for the transfer from airport to port.
