Los Angeles International Airport lies about 24 km from the city centre and handles around 77 million passengers a year. Like JFK, it **has no single main building**: nine terminals (T1–T8 plus the international terminal, TBIT) are strung out around a loop road. The one thing most worth updating here is your thinking: **taxis and rideshares do not pick up at the terminal door** — they all load at the LAX-it lot outside the terminals.

## The nine terminals

| Code | Main carriers | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Southwest and others | Northeast corner of the loop |
| T2 / T3 | Delta and partner airlines | T2 also handles some international flights |
| TBIT | International airlines | Bradley International Terminal, where most intercontinental flights are |
| T4 / T5 | American Airlines and others | Linked to TBIT by a corridor |
| T6 | Several airlines mixed | Linked to T5 |
| T7 / T8 | United Airlines | West side of the loop |

**How to work out where you are:** check the operating airline first, then the terminal number on your boarding pass; international arrivals are mostly at TBIT, so confirm which building your onward flight uses before anything else.

> Our diagram draws the nine buildings as separate structures on either side of the loop precisely to remind you: the buildings are joined by a mix of **walking corridors and shuttle buses**, and the time cost is not to be underestimated.

## Getting between the terminals

- **Walking corridors** — T4–T5–T6 are linked by indoor corridors, and TBIT connects to T4/T5/T6 by an elevated walkway, so some connections can be made without leaving security.
- **Free shuttle buses** — every other inter-terminal move, and trips to LAX-it and the car rental centre, rely on the shuttle; count the interval between buses into your time.

## Getting into the city

- **FlyAway bus** — direct to Union Station (LA Union Station) and other hubs, with plenty of luggage space, and the most predictable option.
- **Taxi / rideshare** — take the **free shuttle to the LAX-it lot** first, then get in; at peak times allow an hour or more into the city.
- **Metro** — still requires a shuttle bus connection at Aviation station; the **SkyLink people-mover linking the terminals had not opened as of September 2026** (targeted for late 2026), so do not take the "direct rail link" claim in older guides at face value.
- **Car rental** — the rental centre is well away from the terminals and is reached by shuttle in both directions.

## Practical tips

- "24 km from the city centre" means very little in Los Angeles — congestion on the 405 and the 105 is the real time cost, so if you are pressed for time allow 90 minutes for the ground transfer.
- For an inter-terminal connection, pick a combination with a corridor where you can (TBIT to T4/T5, for example); for any other combination allow more than 3 hours.
- Arriving late at night, go straight to LAX-it for a taxi rather than waiting at the terminal kerb — only shuttle buses stop there.
