Copenhagen Airport lies only about 8 km from the city centre and handles around 30 million passengers a year, making it the busiest airport in the Nordics. Its structure fits into one sentence: **two check-in buildings, one shared airside area, six piers** — which building you check in at affects only the formalities, while gates are assigned by destination, and the two are not tied together.

## Two check-in buildings and six piers

| Code | Name | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | Check-in and bag drop for most foreign airlines |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | Check-in for SAS and its group; the main arrivals hall and the rail station are here too |

| Pier | Main use |
| --- | --- |
| A / B / D | Flights within Schengen |
| C | Non-Schengen and long-haul flights; passport control required |
| E | Dedicated to SAS long-haul flights |
| F | Low-cost carriers (formerly CPH Go) |

**How to work out where you are going:** once through the shared security checkpoint you are in one and the same airside area, and **gates are assigned by destination regardless of whether you checked in at T2 or T3**. If you are unsure, go by the pier letter: C or E for non-Schengen, F for low-cost.

> Our diagram draws the two check-in buildings as one body with six piers reaching out behind it precisely to make the point: here, you never change building after security.

## Transfers and connections

1. Every pier is walkable once you are through security, and a connection within Schengen usually works in 45–60 minutes.
2. **Non-Schengen flights leaving from piers C or E require passport control**, so add that on top of your buffer.
3. Copenhagen is the Nordic connecting hub, with a high share of through tickets and clear signage — just follow the gate letter.

## Getting into Copenhagen

- **Metro line 2 (M2)** — the station is at the **far end of T3**, about 12–15 minutes direct to the city centre, and it **runs 24 hours**, making it the reliable choice for a late arrival.
- **Regional trains and the Öresund train** — direct to Malmö in Sweden, useful for cross-border itineraries.
- **Taxi** — only about 8 km and 20 minutes or so, and by Nordic standards the fare is friendly.

## Practical tips

- Metro line 2 runs around the clock, one of the few airport lines in Europe that runs all night and goes straight to the centre — **the first choice for a red-eye arrival**.
- T3 is being refurbished (until around 2027), and the central passage can narrow at peak times, so leave a little extra time for the walk.
- Copenhagen is the stepping stone to southern Sweden, and the trains to Malmö use the same station as the city metro — there is no need to find another.
