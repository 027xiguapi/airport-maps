Hong Kong International Airport sits on reclaimed land at Chek Lap Kok. It handles around 55 million passengers a year and lies about 34 km from the city centre. What sets it apart is that **one main terminal carries almost all the traffic**, with two further facilities handling remote gates — understanding that split is what tells you how far you will actually have to walk.

## The three passenger facilities

| Code | Name | Gates | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | 1–80 | Check-in, security, departures and arrivals; every international flight starts here |
| M | Midfield Concourse | 201–230 | Remote gates reached by shuttle from the main building |
| S | North Satellite Concourse | 501–510 | Circular satellite used by regional flights |

**Your gate number tells you where you are going:**

- **1–80** — inside Terminal 1 itself.
- **201–230** — the Midfield Concourse, a further hop from the main building.
- **501–510** — the North Satellite Concourse. The highest gate numbers here usually mean the earliest start.

> Our diagram draws the North Satellite Concourse as a circle precisely to distinguish it from the rectangular main and midfield buildings.

## Transfers and connections

Connections are handled inside Terminal 1, but **if your arrival and departure gates sit in different facilities, allow extra time**. In practice:

1. Work out which range your onward gate falls into (1–80 / 201–230 / 501–510).
2. A connection inside the main terminal is the quickest; a move to the midfield or satellite facility adds shuttle and walking time in both directions.
3. The higher the gate number, the earlier you should clear the transit checkpoint.

## Getting into the city

- **Airport Express** — 24 minutes direct to Central / Hong Kong Station, and the fastest option.
- **Airport buses** — the A and E routes cover every district of Hong Kong. Cheaper than the Airport Express but exposed to traffic.
- **Taxi** — separate ranks for the city and for Lantau; check which queue you are in before boarding.
- **Cross-border ferry** — links to Pearl River Delta ports, useful if you are heading straight on to the mainland.

## Practical tips

- At 34 km out, this is one of the further major Asian hubs from its city centre — build the ground transfer into your plans.
- The Airport Express offers in-town check-in, so you can drop bags at Hong Kong or Kowloon station and travel to the airport unencumbered.
- For a same-day Pearl River Delta trip, compare the cross-border ferry against the Airport Express plus a land crossing before deciding.
