Haneda sits on the shore of Tokyo Bay, about 15 km from central Tokyo, and handles around 78 million passengers a year. It is the international airport closest to Tokyo — the counterpart to Narita, some 60 km away. Its layout splits **by carrier and by domestic or international**: **Japan Airlines group domestic flights are in T1, ANA group domestic flights in T2, and international flights are concentrated in T3**.

## The three terminals

| Code | Name | Main carriers | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Japan Airlines group | Domestic flights |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | ANA group | Mostly domestic flights |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | International carriers | International flights, plus a few domestic charters |

**How to tell where you are:** start from the operating carrier — JAL group domestic flights use T1, ANA group T2, and any international flight uses T3. When you are unsure, go by your boarding pass.

> Our diagram lays the three buildings out along the bay shore as separate blocks precisely to remind you that mixing up the terminal numbers means a long loop right around the bay.

## Getting between the terminals

- **Free shuttle bus** — runs a loop between the three buildings, and T1 and T2 are also linked by a walking connection.
- Domestic to international (T1/T2 to T3) is the most common connection, so build the shuttle's interval into your timing.

## Getting into the city

- **Tokyo Monorail** — about 15 minutes direct to Hamamatsucho, where you change to the JR Yamanote Line; trains are frequent.
- **Keikyu Line** — about 13 minutes direct to Shinagawa, with some trains running through onto the Toei Asakusa Line; handier for points south of Tokyo Station.
- **Limousine bus** — direct to the main city hotels, suited to heavy luggage or a late-night arrival.
- **Taxi** — close as the airport is, a peak-hour run can still take more than 40 minutes, and it is not cheap.

## Practical tips

- For a flight to Tokyo, **the ground transfer from Haneda and from Narita (NRT) differs by close to two hours**, so check whether the three-letter code is HND or NRT when you book.
- The fifth floor of T3 recreates an Edo-period street scene; worth a look if you have time on a connection or before departure, and you do not need to pass through immigration.
- Haneda has plenty of late-night and early-morning international flights, but the trains stop around midnight, so have a taxi or the airport bus as your fallback if you land on a red-eye.
