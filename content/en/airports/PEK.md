Beijing Capital International Airport lies to the north-east of the city, handles around 67 million passengers a year and sits about 25 km from the city centre. Its three terminals differ enormously in scale: **Terminal 3 carries the overwhelming majority of the traffic**, and it is one of the country's rare designs in which **you still take a transit train inside the building** — understanding T3's internal zones is what tells you how far you will actually have to walk.

## The three terminals

| Code | Name | Main users | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Terminal 1 | Hainan Airlines group | Domestic flights |
| T2 | Terminal 2 | Some domestic carriers | Domestic, plus some international and Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan flights |
| T3 | Terminal 3 | Air China and Star Alliance | The main terminal; the overwhelming majority of international flights use it |

**T3's gates begin with C, D or E**, matching three sections of the building:

- **Zone C** — the T3C main building, reachable on foot after check-in and security.
- **Zone E** — the T3E international concourse; after security or immigration you need the **free transit train**.
- **Zone D** — T3D, the concourse between the main building and Zone E.

> Our diagram draws T3 as three parts — a main building plus a remote concourse — precisely to set it apart from the separate rectangular buildings of T1 and T2.

## Transfers and connections

Most connections at Capital are made inside T3, but **if your gate is in Zone E, add the transit train's waiting time to your buffer**:

1. First confirm the gate prefix on your next flight (C / D / E).
2. A connection that stays within Zone C is the quickest; crossing to Zone E means adding the transit train in both directions.
3. Two flights in different terminals is the most complicated case: contact your airline in advance to confirm the transfer route, and allow more time rather than less.

## Getting into the city

- **Capital Airport Express (metro)** — about 20 minutes direct to Dongzhimen, then a change to other lines; a stable choice unaffected by traffic.
- **Airport buses** — routes cover the main districts of the city, and the fare is lower than the metro, but they are exposed to traffic.
- **Taxi** — about 25 km and roughly 40 minutes without traffic, markedly longer at peak times.

## Practical tips

- Beijing has two international airports: **Capital (PEK) and Daxing (PKX)** — always confirm which one before booking or arranging a pickup, as they are more than 50 km apart.
- T3 is enormous: from check-in to the furthest gate, walking plus the transit train can take more than 20 minutes, so arrive 3 hours ahead for an international flight.
- The first Capital Airport Express leaves at around 6am; for an earlier flight, take a taxi or an airport bus instead.
